**Title:**
Figma just rationed your AI access — here's what senior designers need to do NOW

---

**Body:**

Figma shipped per-user AI credit limits on August 5. Admins can now set a credit cap per designer. When you hit it, you submit a request. Admin approves or denies.

Welcome to the era of governed AI design.

Here's why this matters more than the feature itself 👇

**The context:**
In Figma's Q2 2026 results, 80%+ of large enterprise customers were consuming AI credits every week. That's not exploration — that's embedded workflow. When adoption reaches that scale, budget governance is inevitable.

**What changed for you specifically:**
Before: AI was an open sandbox — try everything, fail freely.
After: AI usage has a cost, a budget, and someone reviewing whether your usage is justified.

**4 things senior UX designers should do this week:**

1. **Audit your credit-heavy workflows**
Map out which Figma AI tasks you use most: Make sessions, agent runs, batch image edits, Weave tool runs. Most designers have no idea where their credits actually go.

2. **Build a simple credit-to-outcome log**
For the next two weeks, log: task → credits consumed → outcome quality. You'll find 20% of your AI usage produces 80% of the value.

3. **Document your AI workflow ROI before your next team review**
"I spend ~X credits/week on batch component consistency checks — saves ~Y hours of manual QA per sprint." That's a budget conversation you can win.

4. **Talk to your design ops or admin now, before limits hit**
Find out what limits are being set. Advocate for workflows that have clear business outcomes. The designers who are proactive here won't be the ones waiting for approvals mid-sprint.

**The honest take:**
Some of this feels like bureaucracy creeping into creative work. And maybe it is. But AI credits are now a real budget line — the same way cloud compute or software licenses are. The designers who adapt to that reality will have more agency, not less, because they can make the case for their usage.

The new skill isn't just prompting well. It's being able to justify what you prompted for.
