# Threads Post — Topic 1: Figma AI Credit User Limits
## Platform: Threads | Language: English | Date: Aug 6, 2026
## Source: Figma release notes Aug 5, 2026 — "AI credit user limits and credit requests"

---

**Main Post (Long-form):**

Figma shipped per-user AI credit limits yesterday. Admins can now set a cap per designer, and users who hit the cap have to submit a request to get more.

I've been thinking about what this actually signals — not the feature itself, but the moment it represents.

We've spent the last year talking about AI as a tool for creative exploration, for running more directions in less time, for closing the gap between idea and prototype. And that's real. But at some point, exploration has a cost. That cost is now legible in a way it wasn't before.

Two things are happening at once:

First, Figma's Q2 results showed that 80%+ of large customers are consuming AI credits weekly. This isn't beta behavior anymore — it's baseline workflow. When that many teams are spending credits consistently, governance isn't a bureaucratic reflex, it's a financial necessity.

Second, this creates a completely new dynamic for individual designers. You're no longer asking "what can this tool do?" You're asking "what is this worth spending credits on?" That's a different question. It requires you to have a point of view on your own workflow ROI — which is not something most design education or career tracks prepared you for.

I think the designers who handle this best will be the ones who treat credit management the same way they'd treat time management: ruthlessly intentional about where the high-value work happens. Use credits for the things only the AI can do at scale (batch variations, multi-screen consistency checks, rapid concept divergence). Don't burn them on things you could do faster manually.

The uncomfortable version of this: some designers are going to hit their limit mid-sprint and have to wait for admin approval. That's going to feel like friction. But it's also an invitation to make the case for the work you're doing — in terms of outcomes, not just process.
