# Threads Post — Basedash Chat Dashboard Preview (English)

**Topic:** Basedash launches live dashboard previews in chat — full IA decisions made by AI
**Angle:** The thing nobody's naming is that AI is now making Information Architecture decisions for your data products
**Format:** Long-form personal reflection, no hashtags
**Published date:** Oct 1, 2026

---

The thing nobody's naming about Basedash's new chat dashboards: AI is making your IA decisions now.

Not layout decisions. Not color decisions. Information architecture decisions.

"Create a sales dashboard" → AI decides: what KPIs go on top vs buried, what needs a chart vs a table vs a leaderboard, how to group everything into tabs. Those are real IA choices. And you're seeing the result, not the process.

The interface is smart. The preview renders the actual dashboard — not a mockup of it. You can filter by date range inside the conversation, switch tabs, try different parameters. Nothing is staged or simulated. "Open dashboard" just takes you to the same thing at full size. No gap between what you previewed and what you shipped.

That's a good UX decision. Same principle as Figma Make's canvas rendering, or Elementor MCP's draft-before-publish model. What you see during generation is the deliverable. This should be the standard for agentic tools.

But here's the honest question they don't answer: when the AI decides a leaderboard table makes more sense than a bar chart for "win rate by rep" — was that the right call for your actual users' question?

The tool is confident. The output is polished. The underlying IA rationale is invisible.

Basedash doesn't know if this dashboard is built for a VP who needs to spot a signal fast, or an IC who needs to understand variance across deal types. Both users need different information hierarchies. Both would pass a "looks good in preview" check.

I said something similar about ChatGPT Visualize back in August — AI choosing format for a single data question without knowing whose question it's answering. Basedash is that, scaled to a full dashboard. Every choice is auto-made. Every choice is invisible.

This is useful for teams who already know what question they're answering. One prompt, one complete dashboard, ready to share. That's genuinely good.

The failure mode is teams who use it before they've sharpened the question. The dashboard comes out polished and confident and wrong, and nobody questions it because it looks finished.

The bottleneck didn't disappear. It moved.

Before: "how do we build this dashboard?"
After: "is this the right IA for the question we're actually trying to answer?"

Senior UX value didn't shrink. It just moved one layer up.
