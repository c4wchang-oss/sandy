## Long-form reflection

There's a gap that Adobe's AI roadmap has been strategically avoiding.

Adobe Firefly generates new images. Adobe's AI tools add content to existing files. What they don't do is take a production file that's already 80% done — a 12-page InDesign brochure translated into three languages, a Photoshop retouching pass across 124 product photos — and work through it systematically, preserving every layer, every text style, every character format.

Sekondo, launched today by a 34-year graphic design veteran from Córdoba, Spain, fills that gap.

It connects Claude or ChatGPT to all six Adobe Creative apps: Photoshop, Illustrator, InDesign, After Effects, Premiere Pro, and Lightroom Classic. Not a specialized plugin for one tool. All six. And it works inside your existing files — the real ones you're actually delivering to clients — not a net-new generation environment.

The interaction model is what I want to highlight, because it's correct in a way most AI creative tools get wrong. Sekondo reads how the document is structured before touching anything. It observes, proposes, and waits for your confirmation before executing. That's the right trust architecture for production work. Not "AI did it and here's the result" but "here's what I'm going to do, confirm if this is right." The difference in client work is the difference between a tool you'd use at 90% completion and a tool you'd only use in ideation.

The use case that will convert the most skeptics is the multilingual InDesign layout. A 12-page brochure adapted to three languages is days of work in a normal studio pipeline — moving text, checking overflow, adjusting spacing per language. Seven seconds in the demo. Every text style preserved. I haven't independently tested this, so caveat that appropriately, but if it holds up under studio conditions that's a category-shifting result.

The "use your own AI subscription" approach is also a more honest business model than most. You're not buying a proprietary model. You're using the Claude or ChatGPT subscription you already pay for. The quality ceiling is whichever frontier model you trust, which means it improves every time Anthropic or OpenAI ships a model update. No AI bill creep. No vendor lock-in.

The honest caveats: this is built by one person, so the initial support depth will be limited. The quality of output depends on how well you can brief the AI — Sekondo doesn't know your brand standards unless you tell it. And the impressive numbers in the demos (7 seconds, 1 second per photo) haven't been independently verified under real studio conditions.

But the direction is right. AI creative tools spent two years solving "what can AI generate?" The more useful question was always "how do I get AI to work on the files I'm already delivering?" A practitioner with 34 years of actual production pain built the answer to the second question. That's usually a better signal than a well-funded startup optimizing for the first.
