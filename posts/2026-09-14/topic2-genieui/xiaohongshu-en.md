## Title
This AI design tool treats your design system as a BOUNDARY, not a reference

## Body

A tool caught my attention this week.

**GenieUI.**

One sentence: it turns prompts into editable iOS and Android interfaces.

But the way it works is fundamentally different from every other AI design tool I've seen.

---

**How most AI design tools work vs GenieUI**

❌ Most tools: generate freely → you fix it → constrain after the fact
✅ GenieUI: register your design system first → AI selects only within your catalog → you get an editable component tree

Their tagline puts it directly:
**"A genie with rules is more useful than one with imagination."**

---

**What the output actually looks like**

Not a screenshot.
Not HTML markup you can't touch.

A **component tree** — with layers, inspector, zoom, and direct manipulation.

Because everything was built from your registered components, everything stays editable. The AI is working within your vocabulary, not inventing its own.

---

**Why senior designers should pay attention**

Quality ceiling = **your design system's vocabulary quality**.

- Vague component props → vague AI decisions
- Inconsistent token naming → inconsistent layout
- Unclear documentation → AI guesses

Previous AI design tools could paper over your design system documentation problems.
GenieUI can't.

This tool makes the design system debt you've been deferring visible in a new way.

---

**It's model-agnostic**

Claude, Codex, Gemini, Grok, DeepSeek, Mistral — all supported.

Switch models by task without rebuilding the project.

No vendor lock-in = no single model's aesthetic biases permanently embedded in your workflow.

---

**Honest caveats**

GenieUI is a community v0.1 project, with real limitations:

- There's a gap between "component tree" and "deployed mobile screen" that's still unsolved
- Not a replacement for Figma or your design system tool
- Early stage, bugs expected

But the **constraint-first architecture is the right model**.

The tool that closes this gap at production quality over the next two years — that's the winner.

---

**One thing worth doing now**

Take your most-used component. Look at your props and token names. Ask: are they specific enough for an AI to make good decisions without asking you?

If not — that's a design system problem, not an AI problem.

That answer tells you where your design system investment is actually paying off — and where it isn't.
