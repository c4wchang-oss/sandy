**Tweet 1 (Hook)**

"A genie with rules is more useful than one with imagination."

That's GenieUI's tagline.

It's also the most accurate description of how AI design tools should actually work.

🧵 1/8

---

**Tweet 2**

GenieUI generates iOS and Android interfaces from natural language prompts.

But the model can't invent arbitrary HTML or CSS.

It can only select, configure, and position components from your registered design system.

Your component catalog is the genie's boundary.

2/8

---

**Tweet 3**

The output isn't a screenshot.
It isn't free-form HTML you'll spend an hour cleaning up.

It's a component tree — with views, layers, inspector, zoom, and direct manipulation.

Everything the agent creates stays editable because it was built from your components, not generated markup.

3/8

---

**Tweet 4**

Most AI design tools work like this:

Generate freely → you fix the output → constrain after the fact.

GenieUI inverts it:

Register your design system → model selects within your catalog → output fits by construction.

This is constraint architecture at the generation step, not the cleanup step.

4/8

---

**Tweet 5**

GenieUI is also model-agnostic.

Claude, Codex, Gemini, Grok, DeepSeek, Mistral — swap by task without rebuilding the project.

No vendor lock-in means no single model's aesthetic bias is baked permanently into your workflow.

5/8

---

**Tweet 6**

The quality ceiling is your design system's vocabulary.

Vague component props → vague output.
Inconsistent token naming → inconsistent layout.

The brief bottleneck didn't move. The execution bottleneck did.

GenieUI makes your design system debt visible in a way that was easy to ignore before.

6/8

---

**Tweet 7**

Honest caveats:

GenieUI is a community project at v0.1.
The gap between "component tree" and "deployed mobile screen" is still real.
This isn't replacing Figma or your design system tool.

But the constraint-first architecture is the right model. The tools that win will be the ones that close this gap at production quality.

7/8

---

**Tweet 8 (CTA)**

The harder design system question isn't "do we have components?"

It's: "are our component props, token names, and documentation specific enough for an agent to make good decisions without asking us?"

If not, that's a design system problem — not an AI problem.

8/8
