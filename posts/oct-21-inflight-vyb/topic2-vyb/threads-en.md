I've been thinking about why AI-generated marketing design always feels generic. And I think the answer isn't the model. It's the format.

Image generators are optimized to produce pixels that look like good design. They're excellent at copying patterns they've seen before — which is precisely the problem. Your brand gets averaged into everything the model has ever seen. The output looks like your industry's median, not your specific brand.

Vyb, from Coco Labs (launched yesterday, private beta), is taking a different bet. It's not an image generator. It works in an intermediate format they call Cocolang, where text stays a text object, your logo stays a vector, your product photo keeps its original pixels at full resolution. The output is a stack of editable layers. Change the headline and nothing else in the file moves.

The mechanism matters more than the tagline. Vyb takes your actual brand assets — your font file, your product photography, your color values — as direct input. It doesn't approximate your brand from training data. It composes *with* your assets. That's a different claim than any image generator makes.

Last week, Linearity Vector 1.0 called this the design model vs. image model distinction: image models produce pixels, design models produce working files. Vyb adds a "taste" dimension — supposedly it learns which compositions you'd actually approve, not just execute your brand kit mechanically. That's the harder part to verify, and the most consequential if true.

The Cocolang bet is what I find most interesting as an industry question. Figma has a proprietary binary format that's excellent for visual design but opaque to agents. Elyx (Sketch co-founders, September) bet on a human-readable plain-text format that lives in Git. Vyb bets on a model-native intermediate language that both AI and humans can edit. Three different answers to the same underlying question: design doesn't have a standard format, so what should AI be generating into?

Honest caveats: private beta, no public API, no pricing published, no parameter count or architecture details disclosed. The 90.3% shape match benchmark against GPT Image is promising but test conditions aren't fully published. The real test is whether layer fidelity holds on a complex, mature brand identity system — not a clean campaign asset demo.

But the category question is real: if AI design tools output editable working files instead of flat pixels, the entire marketing design production workflow changes. We went through this with video (HyperFrames, Jitter AI) and with brand design structure (Linearity). Vyb is making the same bet for general marketing design composition.

The "design model" category is forming. Worth watching carefully.
