**Title:** When every platform gets a Connector, the designer's brief becomes infrastructure

---

"Your design file can now deploy itself."

I keep coming back to that sentence after reading about Hostinger's launch this week.

They shipped AI Builder — which on the surface looks like a competitor to Squarespace or Webflow. You describe what you want, it builds a site. Upload a PDF, screenshot, or URL, and the AI reconstructs it. That part's interesting but not surprising.

What stopped me was something called Connector.

Connector is an open API that lets Claude Code, Cursor, and VS Code operate against your Hostinger account directly. Deploy a project. Connect a domain. Update inventory. Draft a campaign. Same external AI assistant. Same session. No handoff.

Hostinger's framing: "A platform is not a platform if only your own AI can use it."

That sentence is the one worth sitting with.

Because we've spent the last year watching platforms become MCP endpoints — Figma MCP lets you run design tools from Claude, ProtoPie MCP lets AI read your prototype directly. Now a hosting platform is doing the same thing with deployment infrastructure.

Every platform becomes agent-readable. The design → deploy gap collapses — not because someone built a better plugin, but because the entire platform surface is now addressable by external agents.

What does that mean for senior designers specifically?

The role that survives this isn't the one that executes faster. It's the one that writes agent-legible briefs — instructions that are structured well enough for an AI to read, plan from, and execute without a human in the loop to translate.

Brief quality, constraint architecture, system legibility. These move upstream, not away.

There's an honest caveat here: none of this changes what good product design is. You still have to know what you're building, for whom, and why. AI can execute the plan. It still can't write the brief that makes the execution worth anything.

But the teams that still think of design as "making things" rather than "encoding intent" are going to find the Connector pattern disorienting.

The ones who've already shifted to orchestration? This is just the next endpoint.
