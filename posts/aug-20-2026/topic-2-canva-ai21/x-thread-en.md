**Tweet 2:**
The backstory: Canva launched AI 2.0 in April. Demand "significantly exceeded expectations."

So much so that they had to pause the rollout. Not because the product was bad. Because serving AI requests at scale was economically unsustainable.

30% growth guidance → 20%. Costs > business model.

---

**Tweet 3:**
What they actually did for 3 months:

Built in-house models instead of relying on frontier APIs.

Image generation: 30x cheaper to run.
Video models: 17x cheaper.
Style transfer: 23x cheaper.

90% cost reduction across the stack. Now: Canva AI 2.1.

---

**Tweet 4:**
The structural insight isn't about Canva specifically.

It's that frontier model APIs as the delivery layer for mass-market design tools is economically broken.

Even at $42B valuation and 200M users, you can't give away AI built on someone else's inference.

The moat is owned infrastructure.

---

**Tweet 5:**
The Figma parallel is direct: Figma's free-cash-flow margin fell from 27% to 14% last quarter — while growing at 36% revenue.

Both Canva and Figma are learning the same lesson simultaneously.

AI quality is table stakes. The moat is who can deliver it cheaply enough to give it away.

---

**Tweet 6:**
For individual designers, there's a concrete read buried in this story:

AI Pass = $100/user/month for heavy AI usage.

The first explicit "senior designer pays more for AI" tier in the mass market has arrived.

Credit governance and ROI justification are now design team skills.

**Tweet 7:**
Also notice: Canva AI 2.1 launched with a new strategy — prefer adapting from existing library assets over generating from scratch.

That's constraint architecture as economic strategy. Less unconstrained generation. More recombination.

It's cheaper. It's also just better design.

**Tweet 8 (CTA):**

When Canva can serve 200M users with high-quality AI at sustainable unit economics, the baseline expectation for every design tool shifts.
