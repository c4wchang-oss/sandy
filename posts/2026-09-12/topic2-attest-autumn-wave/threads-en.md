# Topic 2: Attest Autumn Wave — Research Infrastructure as Design Surface
# Platform: Threads | Language: English
# Date: Sep 12, 2026 | Format: Long-form personal reflection + short punchy take
# Source: https://www.askattest.com/blog/attest-news/whats-new-in-the-attest-autumn-wave (Sep 10, 2026)

---

Your research isn't stuck because you don't have enough studies.

It's stuck because the output lives in a folder that nothing can read.

Attest published their Autumn Wave on Sep 10, and the two biggest additions are infrastructure, not features: a Results API and an MCP server.

Results API means every completed study automatically lands in your data warehouse or BI tool. No more CSV exports, no more emailing a data engineer and waiting for a manual upload. The study finishes. The data flows.

The MCP server is the structural shift that matters more. You can now point any AI tool — Claude, ChatGPT, Cursor — at your entire Attest study history and query across everything. Synthesis across waves. Trends over time. Cross-market comparisons. In one answer, from one question.

That changes the nature of what a research archive is. Before this, research was a series of one-off reports. A study finishes, a deck gets sent, it goes quiet. With MCP, every study you add increases the value of every study you've already run. The archive compounds.

The quality ceiling is still your research question. MCP makes the archive queryable. It doesn't improve what's in it. Vague study objectives flow forward into vague synthesis. Inconsistent framing across waves — using slightly different language for the same concept in different studies — breaks cross-wave comparison. The bottleneck moved from "can we afford to run this?" in 2020, to "can we retrieve this data?" in 2023, to now: "was the question worth asking, and did we structure it consistently enough to build on?"

The push-to-talk interview format is a genuine improvement to how AI moderation works. One persistent critique has been that AI-moderated interviews move too fast — respondents had no thinking time, responses felt reactive. Attest's new format addresses this directly: moderator reads the question aloud, respondent presses to record, presses again when done. Thinking time built in. The format is already standard in other AI-moderated platforms. It's closer to what good qualitative practice looks like.

The senior researcher's job is now clearer — and it's upstream of individual studies. Study architecture. Question framing. Recruitment criteria. Cross-wave taxonomy. Archive consistency. Those are the decisions that determine whether your MCP server returns insights or returns noise. The deliverable got cheaper. The judgment that makes it worth anything didn't.

---

**Short take:**
Your research is only as good as its infrastructure.
MCP makes archives queryable. It doesn't fix what's in them.
Senior researcher role: study architect, not report writer.
