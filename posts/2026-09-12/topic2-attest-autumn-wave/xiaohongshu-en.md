**Title:** Attest Just Made Your Research Archive a Compounding Asset — Here's What Actually Changes

---

🎯 **What happened:**
Attest Autumn Wave, published Sep 10, 2026. Three key updates:
- **Results API**: every completed study auto-lands in your data warehouse or BI tool — no more CSV exports
- **MCP server**: query your entire study archive from Claude, ChatGPT, or any AI tool
- **Push-to-talk AI interviews**: respondents get real thinking time before recording

---

📌 **Why the Results API + MCP combo is a structural shift:**

The old research workflow:
1. Run study → export CSV → email data engineer → wait for upload → analyze separately

With this release:
1. Run study → automatically lands in warehouse
2. Ask AI tool to synthesize across all your studies — same question in different markets; this wave vs. last wave; what changed over 12 months

Research changes from a series of one-off reports to a compounding archive. Every study you add increases the value of everything you've already run, because an agent can now reach back and include it.

---

📌 **What the MCP server enables (practically):**

1. "What do we know about [topic] across all the studies we've run?" → one synthesized answer, not five tabs
2. "How has sentiment around [product area] shifted over the last 4 waves?" → trend synthesis without re-reading manually
3. "What did UK customers say about [feature] vs. Germany?" → cross-market comparison without a separate analysis project
4. Research that was "done" keeps contributing — each new study adds value to what came before

---

📌 **The push-to-talk format change (and why it matters for qualitative fidelity):**

AI-moderated interviews had a consistent critique: no thinking time for respondents. The system moved too fast. Responses felt reactive rather than considered.

Old format: question appears on screen → respondent types immediately
New format: moderator reads question aloud → respondent presses to record → presses again when finished

Thinking time before recording = more considered answers. That's a basic qualitative interview principle — you want the person to have processed the question before they respond, not just react to it. The format is already standard at other AI-moderated platforms. It's a meaningful step toward better qualitative fidelity.

---

📌 **What this means for senior UX researchers:**

1. **The bottleneck shifted again.** Not "can we afford to run this?" (2020) → Not "can we retrieve the data?" (2023) → Now: "Is our archive structured consistently enough for agents to synthesize from?"

2. **Study design matters more, not less.** Vague study objectives = vague synthesis when an agent queries across 20 studies. You don't get a better answer by having more data. You get a better answer by having better-framed questions.

3. **Cross-wave terminology is infrastructure now.** If different studies use slightly different language for the same concept, cross-wave synthesis breaks. Consistency is a technical requirement, not just a style preference.

4. **Research taxonomy is your agent context.** How you label, tag, and structure studies determines what an agent can retrieve from your MCP server. Archive IA = MCP quality ceiling.

---

✅ **How to prepare your archive for MCP:**

1. **Audit cross-wave terminology consistency** — do the same concepts use the same language across all your studies?
2. **Review study objectives for specificity** — could an agent understand what each study was designed to answer, just from the objective statement?
3. **Tag your archive consistently** — by topic, market, product area, time period
4. **Identify time-bounded studies** — research from 3+ years ago may degrade synthesis quality if your product or users have changed significantly

---

⚠️ **Honest caveats:**
Results API and MCP server are early access (enterprise customers first — no timeline for broader rollout given). AI moderation captures patterns and themes, not the hesitation, tone, and subtext a trained moderator notices. Archive compounds only if research was structured consistently — garbage in, garbage out, at larger scale and faster. Attest Explore (AI-moderated qualitative) is not a replacement for a skilled human moderator in high-stakes research.

---

💡 **The reframe:**
You're not writing a research report.
You're building a research library that gets more valuable the more you add to it.
The study is the content. The structure is the infrastructure.
Senior researchers design the infrastructure now.
