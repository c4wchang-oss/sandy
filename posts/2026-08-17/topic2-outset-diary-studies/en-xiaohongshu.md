**Title:**
Diary studies just got less expensive to run. Here's what actually changed.

---

**Body:**

Outset launched AI-moderated diary studies — and the change that matters isn't the AI. It's the removal of the bottleneck that always made longitudinal research hard to justify.

**The old constraint**
A human moderator can only be in one session at a time. Diary study with 40 participants × 4 rounds = 160 sessions to coordinate. That cost — in scheduling, moderator availability, and waiting for synthesis — is why most teams skipped it.

**What changed**
✦ Every round is a full AI-moderated conversation (no scheduling around human availability)
✦ Each session synthesizes the moment it closes
✦ Themes + findings appear from round 1, not after the full sample is collected
✦ Runs alongside interviews, concept tests, and surveys in one platform

**What this makes possible**
📌 Run parallel cohorts you couldn't justify coordinating before
📌 Get early signal from week 1 before full data is in
📌 Track behavior over time without dedicated research ops

**What AI still can't replace**
Human moderators catch body language, tone, hesitation — the pause before an answer. AI catches patterns. Know which type of signal your research question actually needs before choosing your method.

**For UX designers doing research:**
The question used to be: can we afford this?
Now it's: do we have enough questions to make multiple rounds worth it?

That's a design problem. It's a better problem to have.

**Honest take:** AI-moderated ≠ human-moderated depth. Use this for tracking behavior patterns at scale. Use humans for understanding the "why" behind edge cases.
