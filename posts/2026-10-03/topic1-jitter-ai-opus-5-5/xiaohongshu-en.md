**Title:**
Jitter AI upgraded to Opus 5.5 — the model got smarter, but it still can't write your motion brief

---

**Body:**

Jitter upgraded its AI to Claude Opus 5.5 on October 1, 2026. Here's what actually changed — and the one thing that didn't.

**What's new:**

① Claude Opus 5.5 — stronger understanding of motion mechanics: transitions, sequencing, timing relationships between layers, what "ambitious" means for animation
② Every generated animation lands on the canvas as editable layers — adjust timing, easing, effects, and design elements manually after generation
③ Artboard variations — one prompt generates multiple animation directions as separate artboards laid out next to each other on canvas
④ 25% fewer credits per generation
⑤ Image-to-video now inside the AI chat composer — preview, tweak, generate without leaving the conversation

**Why editable layers matter:**

Most AI motion tools give you a finished video. You can watch it. You can't touch it.

Jitter generates a layer structure, not a rendered file. Every parameter is live inside your project. That means the AI output is a production starting point — not a finished artifact you have to work around.

Same shift we're seeing across AI creative tools: Ming-Image Layer decomposes AI designs into editable layers. Mirage Tesseract gives agents direct keyframe access. AI outputs designed for professional pipelines.

**The artboard variations use case:**

Generate 3–4 animation directions from one brief. See them side by side. Apply judgment. Commit to one.

That's the creative review process, not the "generate and hope" loop. Brief quality sets what directions are possible. Your taste selects within the space.

**The honest part:**

Opus 5.5 executes a well-written brief considerably better than before.

It still cannot write the brief for you.

"Ripple effect" and "a water surface ripple radiating from the logo outward at 0.3s intervals with soft easing and a 0.8s decay" are two different briefs. The gap between them is motion direction skill — yours, not the model's.

**The senior UX frame:**

Motion intent documentation has always been a DesignOps gap. Teams spec visual design in detail; motion intent lives in Slack threads and verbal reviews.

Opus 5.5 made that gap consequential. The model is capable enough now that output disappointment almost always traces back to the brief quality, not the model's ceiling.

Write the brief like a motion director. Use editable layers to close the remaining gap precisely.
