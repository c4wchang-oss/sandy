**Tweet 1 (Hook)**
Webflow just launched "Agent Instructions Generation."

Give it your live site → it auto-writes your brand guidelines.

The output will tell you more about your design debt than any audit you've run.

🧵

---

**Tweet 2**
Here's how it works:

AI reads your live site — brand voice, design system components, image patterns, CMS structure — and auto-generates a draft document for each.

Every agent that edits your site will reference these as context.

---

**Tweet 3**
Here's the uncomfortable part:

It reads what's actually on your site.
Not what you intended.
Not what the original brand guide says.

Every override, every one-off exception, every legacy element.

All included.

---

**Tweet 4**
That auto-generated "design system" is a mirror.

If your site has 7 blue variants across 4 components, the instructions will reflect 7 blue variants.

Agents that follow those instructions will faithfully reproduce your inconsistencies.

---

**Tweet 5**
This is accidentally one of the most useful design debt tools I've seen.

Run Agent Instructions Generation not to set it and forget it.

Run it to see what AI will see when it touches your site.

Then fix what you don't want encoded as "brand standards."

---

**Tweet 6**
The governance question nobody is asking:

Who reviews these generated instructions before agents follow them?

"It came from my site" ≠ "it reflects my intent."

Designer's new job: instruction architecture reviewer.

---

**Tweet 7**
What I'd actually do:

1. Generate the instructions
2. Read them like audit findings — what's wrong?
3. Fix the worst site inconsistencies first
4. Edit the instructions with actual intent
5. Then let agents follow them

Sequence matters. Generate → audit → fix → trust.

---

Webflow Agent Instructions, v0 API, CLAUDE.md, design.md.

Every platform is asking designers to document intent for AI.

The designers with the most leverage: the ones who treat documentation as infrastructure, not admin.
