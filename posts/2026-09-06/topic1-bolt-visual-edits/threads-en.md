## Long-form Post

I've been using AI app builders for client work for about a year now.

The part that consistently costs the most time isn't the initial generation. It's the revision loop.

"Make the headline slightly bigger." Prompt. Wait. Check. "Actually a bit smaller." Prompt. Wait. Check again.

That back-and-forth is where client confidence erodes. Not because the AI is slow — but because every micro-change requires articulating it in language, submitting it, and waiting. The experience teaches clients that AI tools are fragile. Expensive. Not worth it.

Bolt just shipped Visual Edits. You click an element in your running app, get a toolbar, change what you need, stack as many edits as you want, and send them to the model in one batch. Client watching live. Changes happening in real time.

My reaction: designers have been asking for this since the first day anyone used a prompt-only AI builder.

Direct manipulation is a 44-year-old UX principle. Ben Shneiderman wrote about it in 1982. Figma built their entire product around it in 2013. It took AI-native tools until 2026 to figure out why "just describe what you want" breaks down for iterative refinement.

What I find interesting is what Bolt didn't ship: no drag to reorder, no layout rearrangement, no multi-element selection. The scope is text and styling only.

"Scope is narrow by design" — their words.

And I think that's actually the right call. Most AI tools try to replace everything at once and end up being awkward for everything. Starting with the smallest, most painful case (micro-revision) and doing it well is better product thinking than shipping a half-complete spatial editor.

The honest limitation: spatial layout is the hard problem. When an AI builder lets me drag elements like I can in Figma, that's when I'll consider them design-complete tools. We're not there yet.

But the direction is right. The design principle is catching up to where it should have started.

---

## Short Punchy Take

Bolt finally added click-to-edit for AI-generated apps.

The UX principle it implements is from 1982.

Direct manipulation > prompt-only editing for any change small enough to show instead of describe.

About time.
