**Tweet 1 (Hook)**
Bolt just added click-to-edit to their AI app builder.

The designers in the room: ...finally.

🧵

---

**Tweet 2**
Direct manipulation has been a UX principle since 1982.

Ben Shneiderman wrote about it.
Figma built their entire canvas around it.

It took AI-native builders 3+ years to figure out why.

---

**Tweet 3**
Here's what Visual Edits actually solved:

Not precision. The revision loop.

"Make it bigger." → prompt → wait → check → "actually slightly smaller" → repeat

That loop is where client relationships go to die.

---

**Tweet 4**
Visual Edits collapses it into a live session.

Click, change, stack. Send when you're done.

Client watches. Approves. Zero wait time.

The UX pattern: batch editing instead of serial prompting.

---

**Tweet 5**
What's interesting is what they didn't ship:

→ No layout drag-to-reorder
→ No multi-element selection
→ No spatial rearrangement

"Scope is narrow by design" — their words.

That's good product thinking. Most AI tools try to do everything and do nothing well.

---

**Tweet 6**
The honest caveat:

Text + styling edits are the easiest direct manipulation case.

Spatial layout is the hard one.

When Bolt ships drag-to-reorder, that's when vibe-coding tools will have caught up to Figma 2012.

---

**Tweet 7**
What this signals for senior UX designers:

The "I can't just click the thing" complaint is being heard.

Visual annotation > text prompting is becoming platform default — not a workaround.

Brief the layout in language. Refine the details by clicking.

That's actually a reasonable split.

---

**Tweet 8 (CTA)**
The design principle that never needed AI:

Direct manipulation > verbal description for precise, small changes.

AI finally caught up to 1982 UX theory.
Now let's see if it makes things feel more designed — or just faster.
