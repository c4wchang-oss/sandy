**[Long-form post]**

The thing Figma Weave nodes actually change isn't the speed. It's who owns the creative logic.

Before: the best AI creative workflow existed in someone's brain — or their prompt history, which is the same thing. Every new campaign started from scratch. Every new team member guessed at what "on-brand" meant.

Now: wire the workflow as nodes. Background removal → brand backdrop → crop to format → export. Share it as a Weave tool. Your team runs it without reverse-engineering what you figured out last quarter.

The Figma node makes this genuinely interesting. Paste any Figma frame into Weave and it stays live. Design system updates propagate to campaign outputs automatically. One source of truth — not two separate maintenance burdens.

What I keep thinking about: this is workflow-as-institutional-knowledge. The node graph encodes decisions. It's more durable than documentation because it actually runs. The knowledge doesn't walk out the door when someone leaves.

But the caveat matters: it encodes structure, not taste. A bad creative direction, codified in a workflow, now executes at scale. The quality ceiling is still the architect's judgment — not the tool's problem to solve.

The governance question nobody's asking: who owns the Weave workflow? In most teams it lives in someone's personal account. That's one resignation away from an institutional knowledge gap. "Workflow governance" is about to become a DesignOps conversation.

**[Short punchy take]**

You're not writing prompts anymore.

You're building the system that writes the brief.

That's the job shift. Figma Weave makes it visible.
