**Tweet 1 (hook)**
Figma Weave just added a node-based canvas.

Everyone's calling it "repeatable workflows."

The real story: you're not writing prompts anymore. You're authoring a creative ops system.

---

**Tweet 2**
Each step in Weave is now a node.

Reference image. AI model. Edit pass. Crop for social.

They connect on the canvas. The output traces back to every decision that made it.

---

**Tweet 3**
When a teammate runs the same workflow next sprint, they don't reconstruct the logic.

The node graph IS the brief.
The workflow IS the institutional knowledge — except it runs.

---

**Tweet 4**
The Figma node makes this genuinely interesting.

Paste any Figma frame into Weave. It stays live.

Update a color token in your design system → the node updates → the campaign re-runs.

One source of truth, all the way to production.

---

**Tweet 5**
You can also branch the workflow.

Try different AI models on the same input. Compare side by side. Commit to the winner.

The decision history stays on canvas. Nothing is lost between iterations.

---

**Tweet 6**
The honest caveat:

The workflow encodes structure. Not taste.

A bad creative direction — codified in a workflow — executes at scale. Garbage-in applies at the workflow level now, not just the prompt level.

---

**Tweet 7**
The governance question nobody's framing yet:

Who owns the Weave workflow?

In most orgs, it lives in someone's personal account. One resignation away from an institutional knowledge gap.

"Workflow ownership" is about to become a DesignOps title.

---

**Tweet 8 (CTA)**
Senior take:

Your most durable skill in 2026 isn't prompting.

It's knowing which creative logic is worth encoding in a repeatable system — and which calls need a human judgment every single time.
