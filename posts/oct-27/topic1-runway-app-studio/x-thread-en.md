**Tweet 1 (Hook)**

**Tweet 2**
What it does: describe the creative app you want in plain language. App Studio writes the code, designs the interface, tests it, deploys it.

Refine by describing changes. No API keys. No hosting to manage.

Included in every paid Runway plan. Uses existing credits.

---

**Tweet 3**
But this isn't about speed. It's about institutional knowledge transfer.

The person who found the right model chain + reference format + prompt sequence is no longer the bottleneck.

They become the architect. Others run the app.

---

**Tweet 4**
Brand Kit as structural constraint:

Apps pull from your Runway Brand Kits and Workflows. Brand consistency isn't a checklist you run at the end.

It's baked into how the app runs. The app can't drift from brand. The constraint is architectural.

---

**Tweet 5**
You can turn an existing Agent session or Workflow into an app in one click.

Lock in the prompt, model chain, and settings. Give teammates a simple interface with only the inputs they need.

"Upload product photo → get on-brand reshoot." No model toggles. No prompt engineering required.

---

**Tweet 6**
What doesn't encode: your taste judgments.

Workflow steps get captured. The decision-making that shaped those steps does not.

If the power user's workflow was already wrong for the brief, the app scales the wrong thing. Efficiently.

---

**Tweet 7**
Real caveat: this is Runway-native.

Your apps, Brand Kits, and Workflows live inside Runway's ecosystem. Strong value if your team already lives there. A real integration question if they don't.

---

**Tweet 8**
The question worth sitting with:

Whose workflow in your team is worth encoding as a tool?

And what gets lost between their craft judgment and the parameters the app exposes?

That gap is where senior designer attention should go — not into prompting harder.
