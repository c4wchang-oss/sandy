Runway shipped App Studio yesterday. The headline is "no-code app building for creative teams." The more accurate description: encode your best workflow as a tool anyone can run.

Here's how it works. You describe the app in plain language. App Studio builds it — writes the code, designs the UI, deploys it. You refine it the same way: just describe the change. Apps pull from your Brand Kits, Workflows, and Runway's image/video/audio models. Share privately, with your team, or via a public link. Included in every paid plan, uses existing credits.

The thing worth paying attention to is what this actually represents. Every creative team has one person who figured out the model chain that works. The right reference image format. The prompt sequence that actually holds brand. That person is a power user — and also a bottleneck. Everyone else either waits for them or accepts worse results.

App Studio encodes that person's workflow as a tool the whole team can run. The domain expert becomes the architect. That's a different story than "AI is faster."

Brand Kit as structural constraint is the piece that makes this interesting. When an app pulls from your Brand Kit, consistency isn't a checklist. It's architectural. The app can't output off-brand because the brand constraints are baked into how the app runs — not reviewed at the end.

What doesn't transfer: taste judgments. The workflow steps get captured perfectly. The thinking behind those steps does not. The power user's call about which direction serves the brief — that judgment isn't in the app. It's in the person who designed the workflow. If the original workflow was wrong for the brief, the app scales the wrong workflow. At production speed.

Ecosystem caveat worth being direct about: everything lives inside Runway. Your apps, Brand Kits, Workflows. If your production pipeline already lives in Runway, this is pure value. If it doesn't, this is a consolidation decision, not a straightforward tool add.

The design role question this surfaces: as a senior designer, which of your creative workflows is worth encoding as a tool — and what level of craft judgment are you comfortable delegating to the tool versus keeping in the room?

The answer to that question is more consequential than any individual app you'd build.
