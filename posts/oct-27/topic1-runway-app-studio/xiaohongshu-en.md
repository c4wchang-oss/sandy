**Title:**
Runway App Studio: what a Senior Designer actually needs to know

---

**Body:**

Runway just launched App Studio. Describe a creative app in plain language and it builds it — no code, no API keys, no hosting setup. Apps use your Brand Kits, Workflows, and Runway's image/video/audio models.

But the headline isn't "fast app building." The real story: encoding institutional creative knowledge as a tool others can run.

---

**Why This Matters:**

① Most teams have a "power user" — the one who figured out the right model chain, references, and prompt sequence
② Everyone else either waits for them or gets worse results
③ App Studio lets that person encode their workflow once
④ The whole team runs the app — no model toggles, no prompt engineering required

---

**What Apps Can Use:**

① Brand Kits (your logo, colors, reference images → structural constraint, not a checklist)
② Workflows (your saved multi-step pipelines)
③ Runway models: image, video, audio
④ Turn any existing Agent session into an app in one click

---

**Built-in App Examples:**

- Character Swap: cast anyone into any video, version for every market
- AutoComp: swap a product into footage while keeping labels accurate
- Product Portfolio: one product photo → polished set of product visuals
- Precise Edit: mark exactly what to change, only that area updates

---

**What to Know About Cost:**

Apps included in every paid Runway plan
Runs use your existing Runway credits (no new subscription)
Building, running, and hosting all draw from the same credit balance

---

**The Honest Limits:**

⚠️ Runway-native only — apps and Brand Kits live inside Runway's ecosystem
⚠️ Workflow steps encode; taste judgments do not
⚠️ If the original workflow was wrong for the brief, the app scales the wrong thing
⚠️ Best value if your team already lives in Runway

---

**Senior Designer Take:**

Don't ask "what app would I build?"

Ask: whose workflow is worth encoding — and what level of creative judgment am I comfortable delegating to the tool versus keeping in the room?

That gap between workflow steps and taste judgment is where your attention belongs.
