**Title:** Notion 3.7 just solved the "one person knows all the good prompts" problem

---

Your team's AI workflow shouldn't depend on one person's chat history.

Notion 3.7 launched Skills — reusable instructions maintained as pages, shared across the team, used by Notion Agent automatically.

Here's what changed and why it matters for designers:

**1/ What Skills actually are**
A Skill is a Notion page you mark as a skill. Write instructions for a repeatable task. Give it a clear description. Team runs it from Notion Agent with a `/` command. Examples: "Turn raw notes into a PRD," "Critique this doc the way our lead designer would," "Format this research summary into our team template."

**2/ The Skills Library**
One hub for all shared skills. Browse team skills, Notion-published skills, your own. Admin sets up to 50 workspace defaults. Everyone is on the same version. No more "can you share that prompt?"

**3/ SKILL.md portability — this is the architecture shift**
Create a skill in Notion → download it → same instructions run in Claude Code, Cursor, Gemini, or Grok. One source of truth, multiple agent environments. The skill you maintain in Notion works across every tool your team uses.

**4/ Auto-invoke**
Notion Agent reads skill descriptions and decides when to use them automatically. Description quality = auto-invoke accuracy. Vague descriptions → wrong skill fires automatically, confidently.

**5/ Honest caveats**
→ Downloads don't auto-sync — you re-download manually when a skill updates
→ Workspace admin controls on Business and Enterprise plans
→ Up to 200 skills per member (50 with auto-use on)

**What this means for senior designers:**
The "best prompter on the team" advantage is shifting from individual to organizational. The new senior skill isn't writing better prompts — it's building and maintaining a skills library your whole team runs.

New org debt to track: Skills encoding outdated workflows, auto-executing for months without anyone noticing. Same as documentation debt, harder to catch.
