**Tweet 1 (hook — attach image)**
It took AI app builders two years to rediscover a 40-year-old principle:

Spatial editing needs direct manipulation, not better prompts.

---

**Tweet 2**
Emergent shipped Visual Edits this week.

Click any element in your app preview.
Panel opens: edit text, color, borders, layout — or type the change in plain words.
Queue everything. Review. Hit Apply.
One batch to the agent.

---

**Tweet 3**
Same pattern as Bolt.new two weeks ago.

Two different tools. Same conclusion:

When you get something "almost right" in an AI-built interface, describing the gap in words is the wrong interface for fixing it.

---

**Tweet 4**
The right mental model for using AI builders:

Chat → layout decisions ("make this a two-column section with a sidebar")
Visual Edits → precision refinements ("this heading is too large, the button label needs to be bolder")

Different modes. Different granularity. Know which to use.

---

**Tweet 5**
The queue-before-apply pattern is the correct UX call.

Every AI builder that applies changes one at a time creates a debugging loop: fix one thing → something else shifts → describe that → another thing moves.

Batch commit. Review the queue. Apply clean.
Same logic as code review.

---

**Tweet 6**
The convergence is now confirmed:

Bolt.new (Sep 6), Emergent (Sep 16).

Lovable, Replit, and Vercel land here within 6 months. That's not a prediction — it's how table stakes shift.

The bar moved from "generates UI" to "lets you refine it."

---

**Tweet 7**
What's still missing across all of them:

Drag-to-reorder spatial elements.
Responsive breakpoint preview.
Component constraints.

Text + styling is the right starting scope. When spatial manipulation arrives, AI builders will have caught up to Figma 2012.

---

**Tweet 8**
The field spent two years teaching designers how to prompt.

Should have spent some of it building the right editing interface first.
