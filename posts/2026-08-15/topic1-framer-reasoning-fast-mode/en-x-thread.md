**Tweet 1 — Hook**
Framer just added two dials to its agent model picker.

Most people are treating this as a speed setting.

It's not. It's a task classification prompt you didn't know you needed. 🧵

---

**Tweet 2**
Here's what actually changed:
- Light reasoning = quick edits, refinements
- Higher reasoning = multi-step work that needs planning first
- Fast Mode (Opus 5+) = deadline speed, higher token cost

Three modes. Three different workflow moments.

---

**Tweet 3**
The question this forces you to answer:

Do you know what *type* of task you're handing to an agent?

Most designers don't. They describe the output and skip the framing.

That's why agent results feel inconsistent.

---

**Tweet 4**
Light reasoning is your daily driver.

Spacing tweaks, copy changes, single-component edits.

Don't overthink the selector for these.

---

**Tweet 5**
Higher reasoning is for design problems, not design tasks.

Multi-route flows. Navigation restructures. Pages where one change breaks three others.

This is where planning before pixels matters.

---

**Tweet 6**
Fast Mode is a deadline lever, not a setting.

Framer makes this explicit: it uses more tokens per task.

Turn it on for client review cycles and launch-week polish. Not for fifteen consecutive micro-edits.

---

**Tweet 7**
The real UX lesson:

Framer surfaced something we've always known but rarely named.

Not all design tasks carry the same weight.

We already adjust our own depth. Now we're adjusting the agent's.

---

**Tweet 8 — CTA**
The selector isn't new functionality.

It's a thinking interface.

If you can't pick the right mode without guessing, the problem isn't the tool.

Your task isn't defined enough.
