**Title:**
After months testing Figma AI agent: the honest take

---

**Body:**

Everyone's asking if the Figma AI agent is actually useful.

After months of testing: it's not the design system *builder* people hoped for. It's something better suited for right now — a design system *janitor*.

**What it does well:**

✅ Audit variables that break naming conventions
✅ Flag detached components and hardcoded values
✅ Document components at scale
✅ Gap analysis to find coverage holes in your system
✅ Consistency checks before publishing

**What it struggles with:**

❌ Creating new components that match your established patterns

Not a model problem — a context problem. The accumulated judgment in your system isn't something the agent can fully learn from structure alone.

**What this means for your role**

You stop being the person who does the quarterly audit.

You become the person who builds a system legible enough for AI to maintain.

That requires:

1️⃣ **Semantic variable names**
"Primary-Action-Blue" beats "Blue-500" — the agent reads intent, not just value

2️⃣ **Component descriptions explaining *when* to use**
Not just what it looks like — explain the use case so the agent picks correctly

3️⃣ **Auto layout on everything**
That's the language agents use to understand your spatial intent

4️⃣ **Component properties that define every state**
Tell the AI the full capability range of the component

**The reframe**

Your design system documentation and your AI agent context parameters are now the same document.

If it's not written for someone to understand in 30 seconds, the agent won't understand it either.

The janitor analogy only works if the space is clean enough to clean.
