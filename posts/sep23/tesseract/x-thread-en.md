**Tweet 2**
Tesseract launched yesterday.

"Think of Tesseract as Premiere and After Effects, rebuilt for agents."

Free local engine. No Adobe subscription. No Captions account. Powered by the same engine behind 250 million Captions videos.

Mac only at launch, local agent environment required.

---

**Tweet 3**
The key design decision:

Instead of wrapping Premiere with an AI assistant that clicks through menus, Tesseract gives agents direct native access to the creative concepts that matter:

— Video layers and compositions
— Keyframes and motion curves
— Masks, mattes, and adjustment layers
— Timing and sound

Agents work with video primitives. Not with a GUI built for human hands.

---

**Tweet 4**
This is the same pattern as OUI-1 in September.

OUI-1 wasn't "AI in a UI builder." It was a model trained specifically to generate UI, with component library signatures as its system prompt.

Tesseract isn't "AI in Premiere." It's a video engine whose primary interface is agent instructions.

The shift: from AI-in-tools to agent-native tools.

---

**Tweet 5**
Here's what this means for the quality ceiling:

When you describe what you want to an agent, the quality ceiling isn't the agent's intelligence. It's the vocabulary the engine exposes.

Does it understand keyframe easing? Layer blend modes? Typography on a path? Mask feathering?

What Tesseract's engine can represent = what your brief can actually produce.

That IS a design decision. The person who chose those primitives made a creative direction call.

**Tweet 6**
The floor movement story is also here.

Free local engine. Agents you already use. No new subscription.

The same pattern as Wixel, Canva, Google Pics: the floor for a category of creative production moves to zero cost for anyone willing to direct an agent.

35 years of professional video editing tooling. Now accessible via brief.

**Tweet 7**
Honest caveats:

Mac only (Apple Silicon + Intel), local agent environment required.
ChatGPT and Claude plugins listed as "coming soon" at launch.
No Linux, no WSL, no cloud execution.
Browser-only chat interface not yet available.

The engine is proven (Captions, 250M videos). The agent interface is early. Check GitHub before expecting production reliability.

**Tweet 8 (CTA)**
Premiere taught you to think in tracks, timelines, and keyframes.

The agent doesn't need the timeline. It needs the language.

The question for creative direction at this layer isn't "do I know the tool?" It's "can I describe what I want precisely enough for the engine's vocabulary to produce it?"

That's a different skill. Worth building now.
