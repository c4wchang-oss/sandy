## 🧵 Threads Post #1 — Long-form, Personal
### Topic: Figma Design Agent — 2-Week Honest Review

---

Been testing the new Figma Design Agent for two weeks. Here's my honest take from 10+ years in UX.

---

First, the quick context: Figma launched its Design Agent at Config 2026 — it lives directly on the canvas, connects to your component library, and can now pull context from external tools like Notion, Slack, and Linear via MCP connectors.

It sounds like a big deal. It kind of is. But not for the reasons most people think.

---

**What immediately changed my workflow:**

Connecting it to Notion via MCP was the first thing I did. I dropped in our PRD links, user research summaries, and product briefs. Now when I'm designing in Figma, the agent has actual context about what we're building and why.

For the first time, "the brief" and "the design" aren't in separate tabs. That alone cuts an embarrassing amount of back-and-forth out of my week.

---

**The real superpower (that nobody's talking about):**

It's not generating new screens. It's eliminating the design admin that was quietly stealing my creative energy.

Bulk renaming layers. Updating component descriptions across a library. Converting 60 screens to dark mode. Standardizing naming conventions.

These tasks used to carve 30-40% of my time. The agent handles most of them now. That time went back to actual thinking.

---

**The uncomfortable truth I tell my team:**

An AI agent is only as smart as the design system it operates in.

If your tokens are inconsistent, your components undocumented, your naming arbitrary — the agent will produce a faster, more polished version of that chaos.

The real investment right now isn't learning prompts. It's building a design system worth amplifying.

---

**What the agent genuinely can't do:**

Make the judgment call when three valid layout options exist and the right one requires understanding your brand's emotional positioning. Know when an interaction pattern technically works but feels wrong for your users. Decide what matters and what doesn't.

That's still design. That's still us.

---

**For senior designers reading this:**

Stop asking "will AI take my job?" Start asking "how do I architect my work so AI can amplify it?"

The designers building the clearest systems, sharpest constraints, and most thoughtful documentation right now? They're not threatened by this wave.

They're the ones it carries.

## 🧵 Threads Post #2 — Punchy Opinion
### Topic: The Vibe Coding Era Is Over

The "vibe coding" era is officially over. What replaced it is more interesting.

Senior designers in 2026 aren't prompting harder — they're architecting tighter. Constraints first. Tokens locked. Accessibility rules defined. THEN AI executes.

The output quality difference is not subtle.

The tools are the same: Figma, Cursor, Claude, v0. But the mindset has completely shifted from "generate and hope" to "constrain, direct, audit."

Figma's Design Agent. GPT-5.6 in Figma Make. Cursor's Design Mode. Claude Code + Figma MCP for bidirectional design↔code loops.

These aren't replacing the design process. They're separating the designers who had a process from the ones who had habits.

Which one are you?
