**Title:** Your brand guidelines are useless to AI agents. Here's what R/GA built to fix that.

---

Brand PDF? AI agents can't act on it.
Figma file? Still too visual.
Design tokens? Getting closer — but not the full picture.

R/GA just launched BRDNA Sequencer: a product that takes your brand's entire design system — components, patterns, voice, visual rules — and converts it into structured context that coding agents like Claude Code can actually operate within.

Mailchimp used it. Their in-house team can now prompt Claude Code to create brand-accurate content without checking every output against a style guide.

---

**Why this matters for senior UX designers:**

The "AI outputs don't feel on-brand" problem isn't a model problem. It's a design system legibility problem.

Here's the pattern I've tracked since July:
1️⃣ **v0 API** — design system as API parameter
2️⃣ **OUI-1** — component library as model training data
3️⃣ **GeekLego / Astryx** — DS rules baked into generation time
4️⃣ **BRDNA Sequencer** — full brand DNA as agent operating context

Each one moves the same idea up a level: your design system is the AI quality ceiling. Not the model.

---

**The audit BRDNA Sequencer actually triggers:**

1️⃣ Are your design tokens semantically named, or only meaningful to the person who created them?
2️⃣ Do your component rules explain *why*, or only *what*?
3️⃣ Are overrides and legacy exceptions documented with reasons?
4️⃣ Is your voice guide specific enough for an agent to apply consistently?
5️⃣ Could someone who's never worked on your brand act correctly from your DS documentation alone?

---

**Honest take:**

BRDNA Sequencer is not a self-serve SaaS tool. R/GA and Addition implement it for you. Most teams can't go buy this tomorrow.

But the proof-of-concept signal is loud: at production scale, brand DNA structured as machine-readable context actually works.

Which means your next DesignOps project isn't "add AI to our workflow." It's "make our design system legible enough for an agent to use."

---

**Senior UX frame:**

Agencies that can translate design systems into agent context have a moat.
Design teams that invest in DS legibility have a moat.

The AI isn't the differentiator here.
The quality of what you've already documented is.
