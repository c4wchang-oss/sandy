**Title: Google Pics just launched — what it actually is (and isn't)**

Google Pics went live in Workspace yesterday. Here's my honest take.

**What it is:**
→ AI image generation built into Google Docs and Slides
→ Object-level editing, text translation inside images, collaborative sessions
→ Available at pics.new for Workspace / AI Pro / Ultra users
→ Powered by Nano Banana image model
→ Workflow: describe → generate → pick → refine

**What it isn't:**
→ A Canva replacement (no template marketplace, no layout composition)
→ A design tool (no from-scratch creative control, no design system)
→ A brand production platform (no asset governance, no systematic identity)

**The distinction that actually matters:**
Canva gave non-designers composition tools — the ability to make design decisions.
Google Pics gives non-designers image generation output — a generated result to choose from.

Different creative loops. Different levels of agency. Different implications.

**The floor shift:**
Workspace has 3 billion users. Every one of them can now generate a visual inside the document they're working in — at zero additional cost.

This means stakeholder-generated visuals in Slides and Docs are now default-possible in any Google Workspace org.

Does your team have a brand governance position on that? Most don't yet, because it wasn't a real scenario until yesterday.

**What hasn't changed:**
Brand coherence over time, culturally specific imagery, systematic visual identity, purposeful narrative design — none of this is prompt-solvable without significant human judgment on top.

**The pattern:**
Canva (2013) raised the template floor.
AI image tools raised the generation floor.
The floor keeps moving.

The question is always: what's above it?
