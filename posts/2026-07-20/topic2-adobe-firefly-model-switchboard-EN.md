## THREADS POST — ENGLISH

Adobe quietly added Gemini Omni Flash to Firefly Boards last week.

Most coverage framed it as a speed improvement. That's technically true. But the more interesting shift is structural.

For the first time, creative teams are being handed model selection as a native workflow feature — not a developer API, not a plugin, not a separate tool. You're inside your moodboard and ideation space, you pick the model, you compare outputs, and everything stays in the same project.

The career implication I keep thinking about: understanding model characteristics is becoming a form of creative direction. "This concept phase runs better on speed." "This final deliverable needs the quality ceiling of a heavier model." That's judgment about tooling, applied at the taste level.

Adobe's trajectory is obvious at this point. Firefly is becoming a model-agnostic creative orchestration layer. Not just an Adobe generator. Not just one aesthetic. A switchboard where you decide which AI runs each part of the job.

The designer holds the switch. That's a different relationship with AI than most people expected.

## 小紅書 POST — ENGLISH

**Title:** Adobe Just Made Model Selection a Design Tool Feature — Here's Why That Changes Creative Direction

---

Last week Adobe added Gemini Omni Flash to Firefly Boards.

Most people read it as "now Firefly is faster." That's true, but it's the wrong read.

The shift that actually matters: **you now pick which AI model runs your creative task, without leaving your workspace.**

---

**What it looks like in practice:**

Adobe Firefly Boards is where early concept work lives — moodboards, direction explorations, quick visual riffs.

Now inside that same space, you open the model selector and choose:
- Gemini Omni Flash → fast, low-cost, great for generating 10 concept directions quickly
- Other models → higher quality ceiling, better for final deliverable refinement

Same session. Same project. Different creative engine.

---

**Why this is actually a big deal for UX/product designers:**

Tool-switching kills creative momentum.

Every time you leave Figma to generate something in Midjourney, leave Midjourney to test something in Firefly, leave Firefly to try something in another tool — you lose the thread of what you were building.

Model-switching inside one workspace means the creative context stays intact.

---

**The deeper shift — model awareness as creative direction:**

This is the part nobody's talking about yet.

If you can switch models mid-project, you need to understand what makes each model different:
- Speed vs. quality tradeoffs
- Aesthetic tendencies per model
- Which tasks each model handles better

That's not a technical skill. That's **taste applied to tooling.**

Senior designers who understand these differences are going to direct AI-assisted creative work more precisely than those who just prompt and accept.

---

**The honest caveat:**

Right now it's one additional model in one specific Firefly surface. The "model switchboard" vision is directional, not fully built.

But the trajectory is clear: Adobe is building Firefly as a model-agnostic creative orchestration layer. Not just their own models. Not just one aesthetic.

A platform where the designer decides which AI runs which task.

---

**Three questions to think about:**
1. Do you know the visual difference between Firefly's default model and Gemini Omni Flash?
2. Can you articulate why you'd use fast vs. quality models at different project stages?
3. Is "model selection" starting to feel like a creative direction skill in your workflow?

If you answered no to all three — worth spending 30 minutes experimenting this week.
