## Long-form Post

Framer shipped Fable 5 and Opus 5 this week. I want to talk about what they didn't announce: the benchmark.

CanvasBench is 236 Framer canvas challenges measuring layout, design understanding, edits, and web design workflows. The primary test is the Navigation Benchmark: take a non-functional nav design and turn it into a fully responsive interactive nav with mega menus and a mobile sliding drawer. It's not a toy task.

Here's what I find significant: Framer defined what "AI design quality" means on a canvas. Not based on general reasoning. Not code benchmark scores. Canvas-native tasks, scored on design outcome.

The model scores right now:
— Sol: 100%, fastest, most complete (new baseline)
— Terra: 94%, efficient
— Fable 5: 83%, leads every model on design quality, handles shaders and animations, proactive (goes beyond the brief)
— Opus 5: matches Fable 5 score at 0.5× credits, 12% more efficient than Opus 4.8

The Fable 5 detail that matters: "proactive" — it sets up styles, reuses elements across your entire site. That's not just generation quality. That's design systems thinking baked into model behavior. Worth the extra credits for the right work.

The practical implication: model selection is now a task-typing exercise.
— Hero work, where design quality is the only metric? Fable 5.
— Speed and budget matter? Opus 5 or Terra.
— Bulk generation? Sol baseline.

I've been saying model awareness is a creative direction skill for months. Framer just made it a data-backed decision. That's genuinely new. Other tools let you switch models. Framer gave you a reason to care which one.

Short take: it's not about which model is "best." It's about which model fits the task. CanvasBench made that matchable.
