**Title:** Motion finally became a design token. Here's what changed in Figma.

**Body:**

For 10+ years, every design system had tokens for color, type, and spacing.

Motion was always the exception. Easing curves lived in someone's Notion doc. Duration values were in a Slack thread from years ago. Nobody could apply them consistently.

Figma Motion's September 30 update changes that.

What's new and actually useful:

1. **Custom animation styles** — Save easing + duration + variable-backed properties as a named, publishable library style. Apply it like you'd apply a color style.

2. **Publish to library** — Your motion style library lives next to your color and text styles. One source of truth for the whole org.

3. **MCP + Skills integration** — Coding agents can apply your named motion styles. Your easing values are now machine-readable. Brief quality for AI animation becomes partially systematic.

4. **Audio on the timeline** — Drag audio onto any clip, trim against keyframes, export as MP4/WebM. Sound design joins the motion layer.

5. **Lottie / dotLottie export** — Export directly from Figma. Dev Mode shows full animation timeline with copyable CSS/JSON/React code.

6. **Text animation controls** — Animate individual characters, words, or lines. Text stays editable after animation.

Why this matters for your workflow:

- "Smooth" and "bouncy" are useless prompt words for AI tools. Named styles with exact easing curves are not.
- Governance finally has a surface: your motion library is now auditable and enforceable.
- Agents can apply motion programmatically: brief → named style → applied consistently = no more per-component guessing.

Honest caveats:
- Still open beta (may change)
- Manually animating never costs AI credits; agent-applied motion does
- Ceiling = quality of your motion style library

The real question this update raises: who owns and maintains the motion style library in your org?

Color → brand. Typography → design systems. Motion → ???

That gap is now unavoidable.
