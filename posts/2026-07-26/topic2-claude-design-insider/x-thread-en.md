**Tweet 2**
Nate Parrott is a product designer at Anthropic who worked on Claude Code's VS Code integration.

By late 2024, the engineers she worked with were shipping "far more than before." She was delivering "at the pace I always had."

That gap is the design problem Claude Design was built to solve.

---

**Tweet 3**
What she built isn't a Figma replacement.

Her framing: "one click above product design." Early ideation. Visual communication. Getting buy-in before the engineers build it.

Not pixel-perfect production UI. Not a final handoff artifact. The work that happens before that.

---

**Tweet 4**
The specific capability that changed her workflow: you hand Claude your brand assets — fonts, colors, components — and it generates compliant prototypes. Every output honors your design system automatically.

Then you share a link. Like sharing a doc. The stakeholder sees it working, not a JPG of it.

---

**Tweet 5**
It expanded beyond prototyping organically.

Slide decks. Landing pages. PDFs. Emails. Animations. Social media visuals.

The pattern: any visual that needs to communicate quickly, iterate rapidly, and be shareable before it's built. That's the real product category.

---

**Tweet 6**
The Claude Code integration is designed around the same speed problem.

You explore in Claude Design. When you're ready to build, you hand it to Claude Code. The design intent, tokens, and component choices travel with it. Engineering doesn't reinterpret — it continues from where you left off.

---

**Tweet 7**
Here's the design insight buried in the origin story:

The bottleneck wasn't the tool. It was the format. A designer working in Figma files produces artifacts that move at human speed. Claude Design produces artifacts that move at model speed.

The tool changed the delivery cadence, not just the quality.
