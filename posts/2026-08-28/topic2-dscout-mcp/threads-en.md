# Threads Post — English
## Topic: Dscout MCP — Research Embedded in the AI Workflow

---

**Long-form post:**

Dscout launched an MCP this week. From inside Claude or Figma Make, you can now draft a study, launch it to participants, and get back findings — without opening the Dscout app.

The headline version of this is "faster research." That's underselling it.

What actually changed is proximity. Research used to live in a separate part of the process — a dedicated tool, a dedicated person, a dedicated timeline. The MCP collapses that. Research becomes something you do inside the same workflow where you're designing the feature or writing the code.

That's a structural shift, not an acceleration.

The same pattern keeps repeating: ProtoPie MCP, Dovetail Channels, Usertour MCP, and now Dscout. Every research touchpoint is becoming an agent endpoint. The question is shifting from "when can we fit research into the schedule?" to "what do we need the AI to answer right now?"

The risk is obvious. Brief quality is still the ceiling. "Can we afford to run this study?" used to be the gate. That gate is essentially gone. "Did we ask the right question?" is the only question left — and it's harder than it sounds.

I keep coming back to the same reframe: AI-embedded research makes it very easy to get fast, confident-sounding answers to the wrong questions. The failure mode isn't "no research." It's "research that felt cheap so we didn't scrutinize it." That's worse. It looks like evidence when it isn't.

The senior researcher role doesn't disappear. But the deliverable changes. You're not running studies and writing reports anymore. You're designing the research system — question architecture, study sequencing, synthesis criteria — that agents execute. That's a harder job. It requires more judgment, not less.
