"Better text" = headline-level type, infographic labels, multilingual short copy.

"Better text" ≠ small text at 1K (still blurry per the model card)
"Better text" ≠ long paragraphs (still poor rendering)
"Better text" ≠ live, editable type in your design file

**Tweet 5**
The grounding feature is underrated for design research workflows.

Connect to Google Web + Image Search, and the model can use real-world references without you providing them.

Useful: "create a 1920s Bauhaus editorial layout — grounded." No reference hunting required.

Not a licence to skip rights checks. Worth exploring for visual research and reference generation.

**Tweet 6**
The division of labor I'd actually use:

Image model territory: hero visuals, scene composition, wide-format banners, illustrated onboarding, visual metaphors, short-form multilingual labels

Design tool territory: interface components, component states, live text that stays editable, brand tokens, interaction logic, anything that gets updated after you hand it off

"An image of a screen is not a screen." Keep that sentence. It still applies.

---

**Tweet 7**
One new variable to understand: thinking levels.

Minimal = faster, cheaper, good for quick visual direction
Medium (default) = balanced for most tasks
High = complex infographic layout, multilingual accuracy, scenes with many references

Thinking level is a brief quality investment decision, not a generation speed setting. What you put in determines when it's worth spending more compute.

---

**Tweet 8 (CTA)**
Nano Banana 2.1 is a genuine improvement for design-adjacent work. Especially infographics and wide-format visual assets.

It is not a design model. It does not produce editable layers. It does not know your brand unless you tell it — and even then, it renders your brand as pixels, not as tokens.

Image models produce pixels — the ending.
Design models produce working files — the starting position.

Both categories exist now. Know which one you need before you open a prompt.
