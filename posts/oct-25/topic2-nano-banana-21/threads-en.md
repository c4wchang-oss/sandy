Every time a new image model launches, someone in my feed posts a screenshot of a UI mockup and writes something like "this changes everything for designers."

Then they're disappointed two weeks later when the mockup doesn't work as a real interface, the text is slightly wrong, the component states never existed, and nothing updates when the product changes.

Nano Banana 2.1 is a genuine quality improvement for AI image generation. Google improved text rendering at the headline level, infographic layout accuracy, wide-format tiling (banners at 1:4 and 4:1 ratios now work correctly), and multi-reference character consistency. The configurable thinking levels — minimal, medium, high — are a new workflow variable worth understanding. The benchmark on Infographic Design is 1048, the highest score I've seen cited for any image model, which is a real signal for UX deliverable work.

But I keep going back to the model card. Because Google published one, and it's honest: small text at 1K resolution is still blurry, long paragraphs still render poorly, left-right spatial reasoning is still occasionally confused, character consistency is improved but not guaranteed.

Those exact limitations are what infographics and UI mockups depend on. "Better text" means better headlines. It does not mean the fine print on a data label in a chart.

The distinction I keep coming back to: image model versus design model. Image models produce high-quality pixels. Design models — Linearity Vector 1.0, Vyb, Canva Craft, all released in the past ten days — produce editable working files, live text that stays editable, design structure you can update when something changes. Nano Banana 2.1 is clearly the former, and that's fine. It just means knowing the division of labor.

Image model territory: hero visuals, wide-format banners, illustrated scenes, visual direction in stakeholder presentations, short multilingual labels in research documentation.

Design tool territory: interface components, component states, brand tokens, interaction logic, anything that gets updated after you hand it off.

The grounding feature is worth exploring for design research. Connect to Google Web and Image Search, and the model draws on real-world visual references without you having to provide them. Useful for historical reference generation, competitive visual research, period-accurate scene creation. Not a licence to skip rights or accuracy checks. But a genuinely different capability.

Nano Banana 2.1 is a better image model. Use it for what image models are good for. "An image of a screen is not a screen" still applies.
