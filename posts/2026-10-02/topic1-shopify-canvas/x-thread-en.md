**Tweet 2**
The whole-store view is the underrated part.

Before Canvas, every Shopify editor showed one page at a time. You built store pages in isolation and hoped they felt coherent together.

Canvas puts every page on the same surface. You pan across them to judge the whole thing before going in to edit.

That's how a designer reviews a store. Sidekick now has that same mental model.

**Tweet 3**
The code-rendering-as-canvas model is the move.

When Sidekick makes a change, it lands in Canvas as the real running code — not a mockup that might behave differently once it goes live.

Full interactivity. Full animation. Key pages with different products and screen sizes.

The preview gap that's always existed between design and launch just closed inside one tool.

**Tweet 4**
The agent validates its own work.

This is the part that matters most from a trust architecture standpoint.

Sidekick takes screenshots of what it built and reviews them before handing the result back to the merchant.

It's doing what a junior designer should do: look at what was produced, decide if it's right, and revise before showing it.

The review loop is designed into the product. Not bolted on.

The honest caveats:
