**Title:**
Shopify Canvas: the AI and the merchant are looking at the same store — and that changes everything about the feedback loop

---

**Body:**

Shopify launched Canvas on October 1, 2026. Here's the UX pattern worth naming — and the caveat worth knowing before you recommend it to a client.

**What Canvas actually does:**

① Every page of the store on one infinite canvas — pan across your whole store before zooming into individual sections
② Renders the real running theme code (not a static mockup) — interactive previews, full animation, responsive views across screen sizes
③ Sidekick (Shopify's AI agent) edits theme files directly — changes appear in Canvas in real time as they happen
④ Sidekick validates its own work by taking screenshots of what it built and reviewing them before presenting the result

**The pattern that matters for UX designers:**

The canvas is the context. Not the prompt.

Same shift we've seen in MagicPath inside ChatGPT (canvas selection = AI context for next message) and Doop's design canvas (canvas = persistent brief). Three launches in one month, same principle: AI stops working from text descriptions and starts working from the same visual surface you're looking at.

That's a different interaction model. And it maps onto how good design feedback actually works: you're both looking at the same thing.

**The agent self-review loop:**

Sidekick takes screenshots of what it built and checks them before showing you the result. The design review loop is built into the agent's behavior — not left as a user responsibility.

More AI tools should design this in. Most don't.

**The honest caveats:**

- Sidekick changes clear your undo history — you can reverse your own direct edits, not the AI's
- Desktop only at launch
- No third-party theme app extensions at launch
- No translation, multi-market, or rollout support yet

The undo-clearing is a trust architecture decision that should require explicit confirmation before it executes. Right now it doesn't.

**The senior UX takeaway:**

Shopify didn't just add AI to their store editor. They redesigned the interaction model around shared visual context — AI and merchant looking at the same canvas at the same time.

The brief is the canvas. Not the chat box.

That distinction is worth carrying into how you think about AI design tools across the board.
