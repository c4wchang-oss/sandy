**Tweet 1 — Hook**

Figma just made a decision that changes what "design handoff" means.

It's called Payload. And it means your design file is now the production backend.

A thread on what this actually changes for how design teams work. 🧵

---

**Tweet 2**

Here's what Payload in Figma Make does:

You describe an app. Figma builds you a website. And now it also sets up: a database, file storage, user authentication, a full admin panel, and APIs.

Hosted by Figma. No separate CMS setup. No backend tickets.

---

**Tweet 3**

The UX implication that matters most: permission architecture.

Designer locks the layout. Marketer updates copy and swaps images. Copywriter publishes blog posts. Each person has a workspace matched to their role.

A design-side governance model, not an IT-side one.

---

**Tweet 4**

This completes something we've been watching build for months.

Figma Code Layers (design → code on canvas) + Specs (agent brief layer) + Payload (backend + CMS) = Figma is no longer a design handoff tool. It's the start of the build pipeline.

"Handoff" as a concept is dissolving.

---

**Tweet 5**

The honest caveat: this is beta and it shows.

Hosting is US only. GitHub/local dev workflows aren't available yet. Not replacing Vercel or a real backend setup for complex apps.

Right scope now: internal tools, marketing sites, early-stage prototypes where a non-dev team needs to manage content.

---

**Tweet 6**

The governance question nobody is asking yet:

When the design file IS the CMS — who has approval authority over content changes? Does a color token update cascade to production?

This collapses design governance + content governance into one tool. The process questions are upstream of the feature questions.

---

**Tweet 7**

For senior designers, the role shift here is real.

You're no longer defining what the handoff artifact looks like. You're defining the permission architecture, the content schema, the governance rules.

That's infrastructure design, not visual design. But it's still your domain.

---

**Tweet 8**

"Prototype to production" has been a promise since Figma Make launched.

Payload is the first update that makes it feel structurally true, not aspirationally true.

What you build in the design tool ships. The design file is the product.

Whether your team's governance is ready — that's the question.
