The thing that's bothered me most about AI design tools this year isn't capability. It's the file format problem.

You generate something good. Then you realize the text is baked into pixels. Changing the headline means a full regeneration. Your "AI-generated design file" is actually just a very fast sketch that you're treating like a finished artifact.

We've been tracking what I call the flat PNG problem for months — Ming-Image Layer trying to decompose flat images back into layers, Jitter making animation outputs editable, Tesseract giving keyframe access for AI video. Each tool solving the same underlying problem at a different creative medium layer.

Linearity took a different approach. Instead of fixing the output after generation, they trained a model — Linearity Vector 1.0 — that generates design structure from the start. Live text. Real brand variable colors. Editable vector paths. Real layers. Not rendered, not baked, not approximated.

The honest scope: this is for brand/marketing design. Social ads, campaigns, banners, print. Not product UI. Not design systems. If you're thinking about Figma components, you're in the wrong tool.

But for brand designers and in-house creative teams? The promise is real. One prompt → editable campaign across every format. Resize to any dimension, translate to any language, swap the hero image, retype the headline. All without a single regeneration.

The caveat I'd keep front of mind: your brand kit quality is your ceiling. If your colors, fonts, imagery direction, and tone of voice aren't clearly defined in Linearity's brand setup, the output will be confident, editable, and wrong. "Editable" doesn't save you from a bad brief.

The bigger takeaway isn't about Linearity specifically. It's that the editable-output problem is now solved across every major creative medium — images, animation, video, brand design. AI outputs are no longer dead ends.

The next question is whether your brief quality can match what these tools are now capable of receiving.
