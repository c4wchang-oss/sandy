# Threads Post — Adobe as AI Platform (English)
**Topic:** Adobe expands Creative Cloud to Gemini + interactive editing in Claude (Sep 24, 2026)

---

## Long-form reflection

Adobe landed in five AI platforms this week. ChatGPT, Claude, Slack, Copilot, and now Gemini. 80+ tools. And somewhere in that announcement, buried under the platform count, was the UX detail I found most interesting: interactive editing in Claude.

The standard AI plugin model is one-directional. Describe what you want → AI executes → you accept or reject. Adobe's new approach adds a second mode. Describe the outcome → AI does it. Or: step directly into the PDF or Express design and make edits yourself — right there in the conversation. Reorder pages. Edit text. Adjust fonts and colors.

That's progressive disclosure applied at the AI interface layer. And it's a more honest UX contract. Because the trust problem with AI creative output isn't "can it do the task?" It's "do I trust it enough not to check?" The interactive editor gives you a middle path: verify and adjust without starting over. Better handshake between human judgment and AI execution.

The governance question is what I keep coming back to. Creative Cloud is now an execution target in five separate AI conversations. Your brand assets, CC library, Express templates — all callable from five different places. Who tracks what was generated? Who catches when the AI's interpretation of "on-brand" starts diverging across platforms? This wasn't a design problem before this week.

The quality ceiling hasn't moved. Adobe Express template quality, Firefly training, CC library organization — still what determines the ceiling. More surface area doesn't raise it. It multiplies the places where the same ceiling shows up.

## Short punchy take (post separately)

Adobe used to bring AI into your apps.
Now it brings your apps into AI.

Creative Cloud isn't the platform anymore.
The AI conversation is the platform.
Adobe is the plugin.

Your CC library is now infrastructure.
Organize it like one.
