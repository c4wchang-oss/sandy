# X Thread — Google Flow Six Creator-Built Tools (English)
**Topic:** Google Flow releases 6 specialized AI workflow tools co-designed with domain experts (Sep 23, 2026)
**Angle:** Domain expert as AI workflow architect — knowing WHICH part to automate is the design skill.

---

**Tweet 1 / Hook**
Google Flow released six AI workflow tools built with actual sound designers, architects, and filmmakers.

The headline is "creative AI tools."

The actual story: domain experts decided which parts of their pipelines to automate — and built accordingly.

That's a different job description.

---

**Tweet 2**
Most AI tools follow the same logic: build something general, hope professionals adopt it.

Google's approach: find someone who does this professionally, ask what's tedious and structured in their pipeline, build that specific automation.

The expert chose the constraint. Not the other way around.

---

**Tweet 3**
SwissFlow Studio converts scripts into Swiss-style motion graphics.

"Swiss-style" isn't a vibe description. It's a specific typographic tradition — grid, type scale, compositional rules.

The style IS the constraint.
The brief is already written into the tool.
No ambiguous prompting for aesthetic direction.

---

**Tweet 4**
Surface maps architectural material textures onto 3D spaces in real time.

Old workflow: source samples → render manually → evaluate.
New workflow: generate texture → see it on the wall → decide.

The mental model changed, not just the speed.
That's what a useful workflow tool does.

---

**Tweet 5**
Mondo Sónico generates background ambiance and foley — and outputs editable audio stems.

Not one flat audio file. Separate stems you can edit in post-production.

The AI output is a production-ready starting point, not a finished artifact.

That's the right product call.

---

**Tweet 6**
The pattern: each tool automates exactly one discrete production stage. Not end-to-end.

The domain expert made a specific judgment: this part is tedious enough, structured enough, that I can hand it off without losing what matters.

That judgment is the design skill nobody's naming.

---

**Tweet 7**
For UX designers:

Which parts of your design production pipeline are tedious-but-structured?

Not the taste calls. Not client negotiations. The parts where you know what the output should look like — you just have to produce it manually every time.

That's what's worth automating.

---

**Tweet 8 / CTA**
The floor is moving.

Not because the tools are impressive (some of them might be — we don't have the data yet).

Because domain experts are now the architects of AI workflows, not just the users.

The skill isn't using the tools.
It's knowing which parts of your work deserve to have one built.
