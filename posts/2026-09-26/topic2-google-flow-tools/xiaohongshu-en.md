**Topic:** Google Flow releases 6 specialized AI workflow tools co-designed with domain experts (Sep 23, 2026)

**Title:**
Who should design AI workflows? The person who knows the tedious part.

---

**Body:**

Google Flow just released 6 new specialized AI workflow tools — each co-built with a domain expert. Sound designer, interior designer, filmmaker. Not "tools for everyone." Specialized pipelines for people who know what the tedious part of their work actually is.

Here's what this launch teaches UX designers:

**① The expert chose the constraint — not the other way around.**
Google didn't decide what to automate. They asked: what's the part of your workflow that's tedious and structured enough to hand off? That question is the right one to ask about your own pipeline too.

**② Style-as-constraint is a real design pattern.**
SwissFlow Studio converts scripts into Swiss-style motion graphics. "Swiss-style" is a design system — specific grid rules, type scale, compositional traditions. The style is baked into the tool as a constraint, not a prompt suggestion. You're not hoping the AI interprets your aesthetic correctly. You're working within a defined space.

**③ Output format matters more than output speed.**
Mondo Sónico outputs editable audio stems — not a flat file. Designed to fit into professional post-production, not replace it. The AI result is a starting point, not a finished artifact. Every tool in this set seems to have made this decision deliberately.

**④ Modular > end-to-end.**
Each of the 6 tools handles exactly one production stage. The expert's judgment about what part to hand off IS the design skill here. Not all automation is worth doing. Knowing which part is the competency.

**⑤ Ask yourself: which part of MY workflow is tedious-but-structured?**
Not strategy decisions. Not taste calls. The parts where you know what the output should look like — you just have to produce it manually every time. Research deliverable formatting? Token documentation? Annotation cleanup? That's the conversation worth having.

---

**Honest caveat:**
We don't have real production data on whether these tools actually save time. The promise is "removes tedious steps, keeps editing flexibility." Worth testing before committing. Surface and SwissFlow Studio are the ones I'd try first — the constraints are specific enough to produce useful output.

**Reframe:**
The senior skill here isn't knowing how to use these tools. It's knowing which parts of your work are worth building a tool for — and being specific enough about the tedious part that a tool can actually capture it.
