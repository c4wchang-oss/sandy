**Title:** Everyone's talking about "vibe design." Nobody's talking about DESIGN.md — and that's the part that matters.

---

Google Stitch dropped a major update this week. New infinite canvas, voice input, Agent Manager for parallel explorations.

The demos look great. The "vibe design" branding is catchy. But the thing I keep coming back to is a small markdown file.

---

🔍 **What is DESIGN.md?**

DESIGN.md is a plain-text file that encodes your design system:

- Color palette and tokens
- Typography scale
- Spacing rules
- Component names and structure
- Voice and tone guidelines

It's human-readable. You can version-control it with git. And — this is the key — every AI tool in your workflow can read it.

---

🛠️ **How it works in Stitch**

1. Export your design system as DESIGN.md (or write it in markdown yourself)
2. Every new Stitch project reads it before generating anything
3. Every screen, component, or layout is constrained to your system's rules
4. Import it across projects — no more starting from scratch

The AI doesn't drift from your brand because it has a file to check against.

---

🔗 **The part that actually changes your workflow**

Via the Stitch MCP server, DESIGN.md travels:

→ Connect Stitch to Cursor, Claude Code, or Gemini CLI
→ Your design system rules flow to your coding agents
→ One file. No screenshots. No re-briefing every tool in your stack

Your design constraints become portable infrastructure.

---

💡 **Why this matters as a senior designer**

Every AI design tool has the same failure mode: brief it, it generates something. Brief it again, it drifts. It doesn't remember your constraints between sessions.

DESIGN.md is a persistent contract. Not a prompt you have to repeat. A file that sits in your repo and tells every AI agent what it's not allowed to do.

This is what "design system quality = AI output quality" actually looks like in practice.

---

⚠️ **Honest take**

DESIGN.md is a Google Labs proposal, not a standard yet. Other tools don't read it (today). The voice features are early and sometimes imprecise.

But file format proposals are how ecosystems shift. If DESIGN.md gets adopted broadly, it could become the machine-readable design system contract that every tool in your stack knows how to reference.

That's worth paying attention to now — not when everyone else catches on.
