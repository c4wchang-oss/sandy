**Title:**
Ideogram Vector 1.0: AI-generated logos finally arrive as editable SVGs

---

**Body:**

Every AI logo exploration used to end as a flat JPEG.

You'd generate a strong direction, spend an hour tracing it, rebuild the layer structure, then do the actual design work. AI handled the fast part. You handled the slow part.

Ideogram Vector 1.0 just changed the handoff.

---

**What it does:**

① **Text-to-vector**
Describe a logo, wordmark, flat illustration, or label in a prompt → receive a clean, editable SVG. Individually selectable shapes. Editable colors, including smooth gradients. Sharp at any scale from sticker to billboard.

② **Image-to-vector**
Upload a JPEG, PNG, or WebP (up to 50 MB) → converted to real vector paths. No separate tracing software. $0.10 per conversion.

③ **Directly opens in your tools**
Figma, Illustrator, or code. No export steps, no intermediate formats. Text-to-vector runs $0.13–$0.32 depending on quality tier.

④ **Ideogram's text legibility advantage, now in SVG**
Ideogram's earlier image model was the first to render readable text in AI images. That extends to wordmarks in SVG — the format production pipelines actually need.

---

**5-step QA checklist before any AI SVG goes to production:**

1. **Anchor economy** — count anchor points on one curve. Clean = 10–15, 60+ = noisy trace
2. **Live type vs outlines** — click on a word. Can you retype it?
3. **Gradient fidelity** — real smooth gradient or banded strips?
4. **Element structure** — can you select the icon without the wordmark?
5. **Brand color edge sliver** — after swapping to exact brand color, check for edge slivers in the old color

---

**Honest limits:**

- Image-to-vector layer grouping "not yet available" — output arrives as a flat pile of paths
- No edit endpoint — iteration happens in your vector editor, not in the model
- Typography may be outlines not live text
- Trademark check required before commercial production use

---

**Senior UX take:**

This closes the editable-output arc across all creative formats:

Raster (Ming-Image) → Animation (Jitter AI) → Video (HyperFrames) → Brand design structure (Linearity Vector) → Physical design (UseIt Studio) → **SVG vectors (Ideogram Vector 1.0)**

Every creative medium now has AI tools that produce a starting position, not a dead-end pixel.

The workflow question has shifted. Not "how do I trace this?" Now: "do I know how to QA an SVG?"
