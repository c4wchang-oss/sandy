**Tweet 1 (Hook)**
Every AI logo exploration used to end as a JPEG.

Ideogram Vector 1.0 just ended that sentence.

---

**Tweet 2**
Ideogram's first vector model ships two workflows:

Text-to-vector: describe a logo, wordmark, label, or flat illustration → get a clean editable SVG.

Image-to-vector: upload a JPEG, PNG, or WebP → convert it to real paths, editable colors, smooth gradients.

Open in Figma, Illustrator, or code. No intermediate tracing step.

---

**Tweet 3**
This matters more than it sounds.

AI-generated brand work has had a fundamental problem since day one: the output format.

You'd generate a promising logo exploration, get a flat raster, redraw the whole thing by hand, or pay for a manual trace. The AI did the fast part. You still did the slow part.

Vector 1.0 removes that step entirely.

---

**Tweet 4**
The economics are genuinely different.

Text-to-vector: $0.13–$0.32 per generation depending on quality tier.
Image-to-vector: $0.10 flat.

Generate a logo direction at $0.20, open the SVG in Figma, swap brand colors, done.

The unit cost of a brand concept exploration just dropped under a cup of coffee.

**Tweet 7**
Honest limits — and these matter before any production use:

Output may be outlines not live text — click on a word before assuming you can retype it.

Image-to-vector layer grouping "not yet available" — you get a pile of paths, not a structured scene.

No edit endpoint — iteration happens in your vector editor, not the model.

Trademark check required before commercial production use.

**Tweet 8 (CTA)**
The workflow question has officially shifted.

Not "how do I trace this AI output?"

Now: "how do I QA this SVG before it goes to production?"

Five-step pre-flight: anchor economy → live type vs outlines → gradient fidelity → element structure → brand color edge sliver check.

Brief quality and pre-flight discipline are now the same skill.
