**[Long-form post]**

Ideogram added context window to Precise Edit. You can now make surgical edits on images up to 8K at their original resolution.

This one is worth understanding precisely — because it closes a real production gap, not a demo gap.

Until now, AI image editing had a practical ceiling of about 2K (4 megapixels). Anything larger — trade show banners, print campaigns, large-format packaging proofs — had to be downscaled to edit. Then stitched back. The seam was always there. The color mismatch always crept in on close inspection.

Context window fixes this at the architecture level. You mark a region (or let the model auto-detect around your mask). The model works only inside that region, at original pixels. Everything outside comes back exactly as it was sent.

For print teams: this is the gap closing. When your composited campaign asset is done and one element needs fixing — wrong copy, outdated logo, color that drifted from brand spec — you no longer have to regenerate the whole image. Mark the region, describe the change, everything else holds.

Honest version: still pixel output. No editable layers, no design tokens, no brand variable connections. Precise Edit at 8K is the best AI answer at the pixel layer — but it's not a design file. Keep your source in Figma or Illustrator. That file hierarchy still structures production work.

What I'm actually watching: AI image tools are systematically closing the gap between generation-quality output and what professional production requires. First editable layers. Then editable SVG vectors. Now full-resolution surgical editing. The "AI can't do this in production" argument has fewer examples every month.
