**Title:**
Spline + ChatGPT: the 3D headline is underselling what's actually interesting here

---

**Body:**

I've been tracking a pattern all year: AI conversation and design surface collapsing into the same interface. Spline for ChatGPT is the most sophisticated version I've seen.

The 3D generation is the headline. Here's what's actually more interesting:

---

**What it actually does:**

① **The real Spline editor opens in the chat panel**
ChatGPT doesn't generate a screenshot. It opens the actual Spline editor — your account, your files, your saved work — in a panel next to the conversation. You can step in at any point, make manual edits, and ChatGPT reads your state before its next action. Your edits aren't undone.

② **Agent self-review with screenshots before handback**
Before ChatGPT hands the work back, it screenshots its output and checks it. The full loop: AI builds → screenshots → you can edit manually → AI reads your edits → continues. Four-step trust loop. Designed in from v1.

③ **2D and UI design work too (Hana canvas)**
Landing pages, app screens, UI components — not just 3D. So the real story is "design tool inside conversation layer," not "ChatGPT does 3D now." That's a bigger claim.

④ **Files stay in your Spline account**
No separate AI artifact. The file saves like any other Spline file — your account, your format, no lock-in. The conversation shapes it. It doesn't replace it.

---

**Honest limits:**

- Quality 3D requires precise scene description — vague prompts get generic geometry
- Plugin is in OpenAI's directory — discoverability is OpenAI's to control
- Requires a Spline account to save
- Hana (2D) is immediately useful; 3D requires domain expertise to brief well

---

**Senior UX take:**

This is the fifth instance of canvas-as-context convergence I've tracked this year:

Doop (Sep 4) → MagicPath (Oct 1) → Shopify Canvas (Oct 1) → MagicOS (Oct 13) → Spline for ChatGPT (Oct 21)

The thesis each one is building: AI conversation and design surface should be the same surface.

Spline's answer to "who owns the design?" is the right one: the file does. The conversation shapes it.

Lock-in lives in file formats. This keeps yours.
