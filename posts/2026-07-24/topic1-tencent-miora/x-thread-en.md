**Angle:** "When AI has a production team, the designer's job becomes creative direction — not prompting"
**Date:** July 24, 2026

---

**Tweet 1 (Hook)**
Tencent launched Miora to the public.

The coverage calls it "an AI creative agent for everyone."

That undersells it.

🧵

---

**Tweet 2**
Miora isn't one AI doing all the creative work.

It's an orchestration layer that routes tasks to specialist agents — brand design, 3D, video, UI — depending on your goal.

You describe the outcome. Miora decomposes the brief and assigns specialists.

---

**Tweet 3**
One example flow:

Describe a product launch campaign →
→ Brand visual agent handles the poster
→ 3D agent builds the product render
→ Video agent scripts and generates the spot
→ UI agent assembles the app screen

Same conversation. Consistent visual system. No re-prompting per tool.

---

**Tweet 4**
The memory layer is what makes this work.

Miora retains brand intent across sessions — style, tone, visual direction. Not reset every time.

That's not a creative tool with memory. That's a creative team with institutional memory.

---

**Tweet 5**
This changes what "directing AI" means.

When one model handles one output, you prompt.

When a multi-agent system handles a full pipeline, you direct: define the brief, set the constraints, review checkpoints.

Same shift from executor to orchestrator — but now at agency scale.

---

**Tweet 6**
The eight built-in skills (brand visual, film, e-commerce, interactive game) are not features.

They're constraint architectures. Each one scopes what the agents can produce within that brief type.

Your job: pick the right frame before the agents start.

---

**Tweet 7**
Honest caveat:

Multi-agent systems accumulate drift silently. One agent's output becomes the next agent's input. Small errors compound.

The quality bar isn't set by what you generate. It's set by the review gates you design into the process.

---

**Tweet 8**
Senior take:

"Creative director" used to be aspirational.

Now it's operational.

When AI coordinates specialists, you set the brief, define the constraints, and review before it ships.

That's the role that doesn't get automated. And Miora just made it more necessary.
