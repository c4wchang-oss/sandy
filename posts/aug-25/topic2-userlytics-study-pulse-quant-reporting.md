# Aug 25 — Topic 2: Userlytics Study Pulse + Quant Reporting

**Source:** Userlytics release announcements, Aug 7–20, 2026  
**The news:** Userlytics launched Study Pulse — a real-time centralized timeline that tracks every stage of a UX research study (setup, recruitment, analysis) and surfaces AI-generated insights once roughly 35% of participants have completed. Alongside it: Quant Reporting, which compiles a study's quantitative data into a one-click, presentation-ready DOC with AI-generated summaries explaining what the data means, not just what it shows. Both join AI Chart Summaries and multilingual AI Transcript Translations shipped earlier this year.  
**Core angle:** UX research's delivery bottleneck just got automated. You no longer wait for the full sample to see directional signals, and you no longer spend hours building the quant report. The constraint shifted: it's not "how fast can we deliver insights" anymore — it's "did we ask the right question in the first place?" Faster synthesis loops mean teams can now act on 35% of data as if it's confident. That's not always a feature. Researcher value is shifting from report writing to study design: the quality of the question, the task design, and what you choose to measure.

## THREADS (English) — Long-form

```
Userlytics shipped Study Pulse and Quant Reporting this month, and I've been sitting with what it actually changes — not just for research operations, but for where researcher expertise needs to live.

The operational change is real. Study Pulse gives you a live timeline for every study: setup, recruitment, analysis in one view. At 35% completion, AI-generated insights unlock automatically. You don't wait for the full sample to see directional signals. Quant Reporting does the rest: one click, and you have a presentation-ready DOC with every quantitative chart paired with an AI summary that tells you what the pattern suggests, not just what the number is. The steps that used to eat entire days — CSV exports, chart rebuilding, deck writing — just collapsed.

But here's the part I keep coming back to.

When delivery is automated, the bottleneck shifts upstream. It's not "how fast can we process this data." It's "did we ask the right question, design the right task, and measure the thing that actually matters?"

Faster synthesis doesn't help if the research brief was imprecise. A one-click DOC with AI chart summaries can make a poorly-designed study look authoritative. That's not a feature.

There's also a specific risk worth naming with the 35% threshold. Early completers are not a random sample. People who finish a study quickly tend to be more engaged, more comfortable with the format, or more motivated by the incentive. Directional at 35% often means "worth watching" — but it can start to read like "confirmed" when it arrives in an AI-generated insight card. That gap needs a rule. Teams that don't have one will act confidently on incomplete data, just faster.

The senior researcher role is genuinely shifting. Less transcript review. Less chart building. More study architecture: deciding what to run, how to frame tasks, which signals to surface to which stakeholders, and how to brief the AI layer so it's interpreting against the right context.

The person who can write a precise research brief is the new constraint. The tool can deliver. The question is whether you gave it the right job to do.

Honest note: the AI summaries in Quant Reporting are currently in beta. The interpretation layer — "what it means" — is the part most likely to improve (and most worth validating before you forward the DOC to leadership).
```
