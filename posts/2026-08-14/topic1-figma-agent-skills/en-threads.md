# Format: Long-form personal reflection + punchy closing take

---

**Long-form post:**

Figma launched community skills for the agent today.

You can now publish your design workflows as slash commands. Other designers can find them, use them, remix them.

The library already has 50+ skills. Design systems audits, research frameworks, handoff checklists. Stuff that used to live in Notion docs nobody reads.

My first reaction was: this is just commoditization. The good skills will get used by everyone and the advantage disappears.

Then I realized I had the frame backwards.

A skill isn't a shortcut. It's a position. It's the answer to "how should this work?" encoded into something executable. The skill that gets copied across 1000 teams still started with one designer who had a clear enough point of view to write it down precisely.

The designers who benefit here aren't the ones who find the best skill in the library. They're the ones whose skill ends up in the library.

That's the inversion. You stop being the person who does the critique. You become the person who designed the critique.

**Punchy follow-up:**

Ten years ago, your design system scaled your output.
Skills scale your judgment.
Same concept. Much higher ceiling.
