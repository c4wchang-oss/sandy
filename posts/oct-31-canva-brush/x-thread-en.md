# X / Twitter Thread — Canva Brush (EN)
# Oct 31, 2026 | Senior UX Designer POV

---

**Tweet 1 (hook)**
Canva just shipped a vision-language model for design editing.

Not image generation. Editing.

This is a different product category.

---

**Tweet 2**
Canva Brush sees your existing design, understands its structure, and makes the changes you asked for without touching what you didn't ask it to touch.

That isolation has been the missing piece in every AI design tool so far.

---

**Tweet 3**
The mechanism: an agentic loop.

Writes code to adjust elements → inspects the page → decides the next step.

The self-review loop isn't bolted on as a feature. It's how the model works.

---

**Tweet 4**
Craft generates. Brush edits.

Two in-house models, two clearly scoped jobs.

That's product thinking, not feature thinking.

---

**Tweet 5**
The reinforcement learning setup: reward system defined by professional designers at Canva.

"Good design" is not a pixel metric. It requires taste in the loop.

Whether RL actually captured that taste at scale — we'll see.

---

**Tweet 6**
Every output is fully editable.

Text, layout, colors — all yours to modify after Brush finishes.

Editable AI output is table stakes now. But getting it right at the editing layer, not just generation, is harder. Brush is the first VLM to attempt it at Canva's scale.

---

**Tweet 7**
Honest read:

- Pro-only at launch
- No API yet
- Real test isn't "can it improve a slide deck" — it's "can it edit a brand-constrained multi-section product page without introducing visual drift"

That question isn't answered in the announcement.

---

**Tweet 8**
The deeper shift:

Canva now has in-house models for both generation (Craft) AND editing (Brush).

Both trained on real design tasks. Both with designers defining what quality means.

The model is shaped by professional judgment. Not just user volume.
