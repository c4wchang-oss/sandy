**Tweet 1 (Hook)**
Ovren just launched "Live Preview" today. Describe a website change in plain words. It builds it on a safe copy. You click through the actual page. Then approve or push back.

This is quietly a design discipline question disguised as a developer tool.

---

**Tweet 2**
Here's the problem it's naming:

As coding agents replace human developers as executors, the deployment approval step changes shape.

Human developer iteration: forgiving. You clarify in real time.

Agent execution: every ambiguous approval is an instruction.

---

**Tweet 3**
"Add a customer reviews section under the banner."

That's your instruction. The specificity of that sentence determines what ships to production.

Ovren's model: agent makes the change on a safe copy → you interact with the real page → approve or push back with a note → then it publishes.

---

**Tweet 4**
This is the same pattern I called out with Inflight (Oct 21):

As the executor becomes an AI, the cost of imprecise feedback and approval scales.

Inflight structured design review comments before the agent executes. Ovren structures the approval gate before the agent publishes.

---

**Tweet 5**
The interaction model is worth noting:

"Click around it like a visitor would."

This is usability validation baked into the approval step — not a screenshot, not a preview image, but the actual interactive page.

That's a higher bar than most design review tools set.

---

**Tweet 6**
Ovren's framing is deliberately non-technical:

Plain language change description. "Approve" or "send it back with a note." No developer terminology in the approval loop.

This is a brief quality problem wearing a deployment tool's clothes.

---

**Tweet 7**
Honest caveats:

Limited to what's visible on the page. Behind-the-scenes changes get a plain-language report, not a live preview.

And the model only works if the change description was precise enough to begin with.

---

**Tweet 8 (CTA)**
The question this surfaces: who in your org writes precise change descriptions for AI-executed website updates?

Not a developer role. Not a PM role. Somewhere in design × content governance — and most orgs don't have that person yet.

That gap gets more expensive as AI executes more.
