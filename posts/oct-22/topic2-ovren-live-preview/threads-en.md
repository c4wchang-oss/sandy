Ovren launched Live Preview today. The description is simple: write what you want to change in plain language. It builds the change on a safe copy of your site. You click around the actual page. Then you approve or push back with a note.

I almost scrolled past it. Then I thought about what the approval model is actually saying.

As coding agents take over more execution work, the deployment gate is becoming a different kind of design surface. When a human developer made a change, the review loop was forgiving — you could iterate, ask questions, clarify intent in real time. When an agent executes a change, every decision you make in the approval loop is effectively a brief.

"Add a customer reviews section under the banner." That's your instruction. The specificity of that sentence determines what ships to production.

What Ovren is betting: the person approving the change needs to see the actual interactive result, not a mockup, not a screenshot. They need to click through it like a visitor. That's a higher bar than most design review or preview tools set.

I wrote about Inflight yesterday — a tool that structures design review comments before a coding agent executes them. Ovren is the same logic one step later in the pipeline: structure the approval gate before the agent publishes.

The honest limitation: it only previews what's visible on the page. Backend changes get a plain-language report. And the model only works if you wrote a precise enough change description in the first place.

That's the real gap Ovren surfaces: who in your organization is responsible for writing precise change descriptions for AI-executed website updates? Not a developer role. Not a PM role. It's somewhere in the overlap between design governance and content strategy, and most orgs don't have someone doing it.

That gap is going to get more expensive as AI executes more of the implementation work.
