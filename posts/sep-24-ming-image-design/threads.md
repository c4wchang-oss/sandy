# Threads Post — Ming-Image-0.1-Design
**Date:** Sep 24, 2026
**Topic:** The flat PNG problem — and why the Layer model matters
**Format:** Long-form personal reflection
**Language:** English
**Note:** No hashtags. Personal/reflective tone.

---

There is a problem I have been calling the flat PNG problem for about two years, and until this week I had no good answer to it.

Every AI image generation tool — the mainstream ones, the design-specialized ones, all of them — gives you a single flattened output. If you generated something good, you can try to describe it again more precisely, or take it into Photoshop and try to fix it manually. But the thing the AI made is not editable in any meaningful structural sense. You get a picture of a design, not a design.

Ant Group's inclusionAI lab shipped two models on Monday that are the first serious attempt I've seen in the open-source space to address this.

Ming-Image-0.1-Design generates full-page UI screens, dashboards, infographics, and posters with readable text. That last part — readable text — is still not solved well in most image models, and it matters a lot for anything remotely design-adjacent.

Ming-Image-0.1-Design-Layer is the more interesting model. You pass it a generated design and a layer plan: tell it how many layers you want and what they correspond to (text, cards, main elements, background). It splits the flat image into separate transparent PNGs — something closer to what a design file looks like before you flatten it to export.

Is it a Figma file? No. Can you push a button and get editable Figma layers? No. But getting separate transparent layers from a generated design is genuinely novel in the open-weight space, and I think it opens up workflows that weren't practical before.

The use case I keep coming back to is not the one the press release describes. It's not "designers generating mockups." It's the three-person marketing team that turns out sixty social assets a week, all slightly different, none of them worth the time it takes to open Figma. Volume production of functional visual content where the brief is a paragraph and the output needs to be editable enough to swap a headline.

The MIT license is significant here. Ant Group — the Alipay parent — released these commercially free. They run via OpenRouter at no cost. The floor for that production-asset workflow just moved.

Two things worth being honest about.

First: the benchmark claim (top of the open-weight UI/UX Design leaderboard on Artificial Analysis) is presented as a chart image with no actual numbers in the model card. I cannot independently verify the ranking or the magnitude of the lead over other models. Test it on your use case before you trust it.

Second: the Layer model is genuinely interesting, but I haven't seen documentation on how well the layer separation holds up on complex designs, on layouts with lots of overlapping elements, or on designs generated by tools other than Ming-Image-0.1-Design itself. The claimed range is 2 to 9 layers. That's a wide range with no guidance on when you'd use each end of it.

The PPT Skill — which converts a flat design image into an editable PPTX — is the thing in this release that will get the least attention and probably see the most practical use. The friction between "AI generates something that looks like a slide" and "I can actually edit this slide" has been persistent. If that friction is genuinely reduced, a lot of research reporting workflows will change.

I'll be honest: I'm going to test this before I recommend it to anyone. But "editable layers from a text prompt" is a sentence worth sitting with.
