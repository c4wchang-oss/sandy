# Topic 2: Krea 3 + Krea Agents (Aug 28, 2026)

**Source:** https://blockchain.news/ainews/krea-3-debuts-with-powerful-editing-agents-platform | https://www.krea.ai/mcp | https://www.krea.ai/blog/ai-workflow-agent

**Senior UX angle:**
Krea shipped two things at once: Krea 3 (new foundation model with advanced editing + aesthetic diversity) and Krea Agents (a platform of interfaces specifically designed for agentic creative workflows, with MCP and API). The detail that matters: the Node Agent shows you a plan before it touches anything. Four stages: prompt refinement → model selection → generation → enhancement → output. You can swap models or drop stages before it starts. That's not just a safety feature — that's the right interaction model for agentic creative tools. Krea's differentiator has always been aesthetic quality over raw capability. The question Krea 3 raises: when the platform aesthetic is the model's bias, where is the designer's taste?

## Threads Post (English)

Krea shipped two things this week: Krea 3, a new foundation model with advanced editing and broader aesthetic diversity, and Krea Agents — a platform of interfaces designed specifically for agentic creative workflows. MCP and API.

The detail that matters isn't the model quality. It's the Node Agent's interaction model.

You describe what you want to make. It reads your canvas, plans a pipeline (prompt refinement → model selection → generation → enhancement), and shows you the plan before it starts building. You can swap models or drop stages before it runs.

That's the right design. Not "here's what the agent did." Not "the agent guessed what you wanted." Show the plan. Get approval. Execute. That's how creative tools earn designer trust.

Krea's always had a clear positioning: aesthetic quality as the differentiator, not raw generation speed. Krea 3 extends that into a platform with agentic interfaces. The bet: when generation capability is commoditized, taste-at-the-model-level becomes the moat.

The honest question it leaves me with: when the platform's aesthetic is the model's bias — when Krea's visual sensibility is built into how it generates — where exactly is the designer's taste in the output? I'm not saying that's a dealbreaker. But it's the question every senior designer should be asking every time they let a model with strong aesthetic training make visual decisions.

The "show plan before executing" pattern is something we should be designing into every agentic creative tool. Every workflow that touches creative decisions should have a moment where the agent says: here's what I'll do, here's what I'll touch, confirm before I run.

That's not a safety feature. That's the interaction model.
