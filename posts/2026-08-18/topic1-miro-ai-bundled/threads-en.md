# Topic: Miro Enterprise AI Bundling (Aug 17, 2026)

---

**[Long-form reflection]**

Miro just made AI Workflows and Prototypes standard in enterprise licenses. No extra cost. Just included.

I've been watching enterprise SaaS pricing long enough to know: when a vendor stops charging separately for a feature, it means one of two things. Either the feature isn't converting as an add-on, or it's become so fundamental to the product's value story that separating it out creates friction.

This feels like the second one.

The "AI as a premium tier" era in design tooling is basically over. Figma, Miro, Adobe — they're not asking enterprise orgs to opt into AI anymore. They're asking enterprise admins to govern it.

That's actually a more interesting challenge than access.

When I talk to design teams about AI tools, the conversation is almost never "we can't afford it." It's "we're not sure who owns the output," "we don't know what data we're sending," or "our legal team hasn't approved it yet."

Bundling makes all of those questions urgent, not optional.

The constraint architecture problem used to live in design systems. Now it lives at the org level. Senior designers who understand both are going to be very useful in the next 12 months — not because they know how to use AI, but because they know how to define when and where AI belongs in the workflow.

Access is solved. Intent isn't.

---

**[Short punchy take]**

Miro just made AI the default, not the upgrade.

The question isn't whether your team uses AI anymore.
It's whether your team has a policy for it.
