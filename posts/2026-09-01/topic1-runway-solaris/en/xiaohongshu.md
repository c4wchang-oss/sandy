**Title:**
Runway just proved your design handoff was always lossy

---

**Body:**

Every interface shipped is a lossy compression.

Not a metaphor — Runway measured it with Solaris, their new Interface World Model announced Aug 31.

Here's what it means for senior UX designers 👇

**1/ THE FINDING**
Runway tested top multimodal LLMs (Claude Fable 5 included) on recreating interfaces from screenshots.
Result: SSIM drops consistently as visual complexity increases. Every model. Every time.
The richer your design intent, the more gets lost in translation.

**2/ WHAT SOLARIS DOES**
→ Generates UI frame-by-frame in real time
→ No intermediate code layer — the frame IS the interface
→ User study (250 participants, 7,500 judgments): preferred over coded interfaces 61% on instruction following, 71% on natural behavior

**3/ WHAT IT STILL CAN'T DO**
→ Stable, legible text (hardest problem in video generation)
→ Accessibility APIs (screen readers, ARIA)
→ Long-session coherence
→ Backend integration
These gaps = where UX precision still lives

**4/ THE IMPLICATION FOR YOUR ROLE**
If code is no longer the intermediate layer, the designer's artifact shifts:
→ Intent documentation
→ Constraint architecture
→ Natural language rules describing what interactions mean

Because that's now what the model trains on.

**5/ HONEST TAKE**
Solaris = early access only, research preview. Not a product yet.
But the measurement of handoff information loss IS the finding.
We always knew it was lossy. Now it's quantified.

**6/ SENIOR DESIGNER REFRAME**
Your value was never in the pixels.
It was in understanding what gets lost in every translation.
The layer moved. Your job didn't.
