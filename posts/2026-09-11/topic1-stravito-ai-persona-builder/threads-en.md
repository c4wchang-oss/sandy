The trust problem with AI personas was never capability.

It was always: "where did this come from?"

Stravito AI Persona Builder, launched this week, builds personas from your company's own research — studies, interviews, transcripts, collections. Every trait is scored and linked to its exact source document. Contested traits — where your own research contradicts itself — are flagged, not silently averaged.

Where the research doesn't cover a topic, the persona says so. That's not a default LLM behavior. That's an explicit design decision. Most tools do the opposite: fill gaps with plausible-sounding invention.

The quality ceiling is your research corpus. Not the model. Not the platform.

I've been saying for months that AI output quality = context quality. This is that principle applied to the persona layer. Thin research → thin personas. Biased sampling in your archive → biased traits with source citations you can actually trace. Recent research outweighs old — recency is tracked and visible.

This doesn't change what the work is. It changes who curates it.

Before: "How do I create better personas?"
Now: "How do I maintain a research archive clean enough to build reliable personas from?"

The shift is from creation to curation. That's actually closer to what senior researchers do anyway — synthesis, not fabrication. The generation is fast. The judgment about what research is worth including is still yours.

Honest caveats: Q4 2026 for GA — early access only right now. Source-traceability doesn't fix sampling bias, it makes bias auditable, which is better but not the same thing. Primary audience is marketing and insights teams. This isn't a product design tool yet, and it's not a replacement for talking to actual users.

---

**Short take:**
AI personas have always had a trust problem.
Stravito built the citation layer.
Your research corpus is still the ceiling.
