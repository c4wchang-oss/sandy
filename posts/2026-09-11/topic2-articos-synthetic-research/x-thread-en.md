**Tweet 3**
Why does a published methodology matter?

Most AI research tools say they're accurate.
They don't define "accurate." They don't say what they're measuring against.

Articos measured theme recall: how many findings human researchers found, Articos also found.
Benchmark: NNG and Baymard published research.

That's a checkable claim.

---

**Tweet 4**
The methodology design is worth noting:

→ Hypothesis-blind interviews: each persona answers in isolation without knowing your hypothesis
→ 12–50 AI personas per study, each with Big Five personality traits + domain context
→ Adversarial review built in — skeptics and blockers in every panel by default
→ Evidence chains: every finding links to a specific persona quote

Structured protocol, not just "ask the AI."

---

**Tweet 5**
Honest reading of "86% theme recall":

It means Articos surfaces 86% of themes expert human researchers found.

It does NOT mean:
→ 86% accuracy on emotional nuance
→ 86% at discovering unknown unknowns
→ Works equally well across all 9 domains (per-domain scores vary)
→ Replaces moderated interviews for high-stakes UX decisions

The right use case matters enormously.

---

**Tweet 6**
The correct frame: triage tool, not replacement.

Best fit:
→ Concept validation before investing in real recruitment
→ Message testing across multiple variants at scale
→ Directional signal: "is this worth continuing?"

Not suited for:
→ Discovery research (finding what you didn't know to ask)
→ High-stakes decisions with real user consequences
→ Emotional nuance and cultural edge cases

---

**Tweet 7**
Live Calls is the methodological gap it closes:

Before: written follow-up questions with AI personas (Talk to Research)
Now: real-time voice conversation with simulated personas

Written prompts don't replicate conversation dynamics.
For probing deeper on a finding, voice changes what you can learn.
