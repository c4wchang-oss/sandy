**Title:** Rivet: AI Gives You 30 Directions. You Pick the Winner. 🎨

---

🎨 **What It Is**

Rivet (YC F25) is a visual AI editor that plugs into your existing coding agents — Cursor, Claude, Codex.

Not a standalone design tool. A visual exploration layer that builds dozens of real, working interface directions in parallel from your current agent.

---

🔧 **How It Works**

① Connect your agent (Cursor / Claude / Codex)
② Give a prompt + design references (Pinterest, Are.na, local files)
③ Rivet builds multiple directions in isolated branches of your actual project
④ Each direction = real code running in your browser
⑤ Compare side-by-side → pick the best → commit as a normal PR

---

💡 **What This Means for Designers**

The old bottleneck: bold exploration was too expensive.

One direction took 2–3 days. So we all learned to pre-filter — one safe direction per meeting. We called it "being strategic." It was risk management.

Rivet removes that cost.

Exploring a wrong direction now costs nothing. Which makes bold directions rational again.

---

🎯 **The Part Worth Paying Attention To**

Reference ingestion.

The Pinterest boards, Are.na collections, local files you feed Rivet — those become your context window.

Design judgment moves upstream: into what you choose to reference and how you prompt.

The designer who curates better references and prompts more precisely wins on output quality — not speed.

---

⚡ **An Honest Note**

Picking the best direction out of 30 isn't simpler than picking 1.

The bottleneck doesn't disappear — it shifts. From execution to judgment.

That's the senior designer's actual job. The tool generates. It can't choose for you.

---

📌 **Who Should Try It**

✅ Design engineers working in Cursor / Claude Code / Codex
✅ Product designers who want to explore more directions before committing
✅ Freelance designers who need to pitch multiple visual directions fast
