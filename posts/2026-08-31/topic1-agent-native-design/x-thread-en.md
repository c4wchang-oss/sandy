**Tweet 1 (hook)**
The handoff problem isn't a process problem. It's an artifact problem.

When the prototype is a picture of a screen, friction is structural.

**Tweet 2**
Builder.io shipped Agent Native Design last month. MIT-licensed. No signup. Free.

The artifact you produce isn't a mockup — it's a real HTML page running in a real browser.

**Tweet 3**
You attach your design system (Figma file, repo URL, or package), and their Design System Intelligence indexes your components, tokens, and icons.

The agent generates from your constraints — not in some generic AI house style.

**Tweet 4**
What this changes at the ideation stage:

Responsive failures. Interaction dead ends. Empty states.

These usually surface at staging, weeks after decisions were locked. With a live HTML prototype, they surface in week one.

**Tweet 5**
The interaction model is right:

Describe the screen → AI generates HTML with Alpine + Tailwind → refine by talking to it

Export HTML, ZIP, PDF, or Figma SVG. Same source the preview rendered.

**Tweet 6**
Honest caveat: this isn't Figma.

Complex component systems, design system governance, enterprise-scale handoff — not there yet.

It lives at the ideation-to-browser-validation stage.

**Tweet 7**
But the conceptual reframe is real:

When the prototype IS the deliverable — the same HTML that hands off to a coding agent — "does it work?" becomes a week-one question. Not a week-six surprise.

**Tweet 8**
The senior designer question:

Are you still handing off a picture of a screen, or the screen itself?
