**Title:** Maze AI just collapsed the 2-phase research sprint into 1

---

For years, unmoderated UX research had one rule: you catch *what* people do, never *why*. That just changed.

Maze launched AI Conversation Blocks. Here's what it actually means for how you run research.

🔍 What's the real problem being solved

The "what" and "why" gap in unmoderated studies was always a timing problem. By the time you ran a qualitative follow-up interview, the participant's memory had already faded. You were asking them to reconstruct a feeling they'd long moved past.

AI conversation blocks catch "why" while the friction is still live in working memory.

🧩 What it does (the actual mechanics)

① Insert a conversation block anywhere in your study — after a task, a rating, a misclick, or a drop-off point

② The AI conducts an adaptive follow-up conversation with each participant

③ Conditional logic routes different questions based on what each person says — no identical interview for everyone

④ Combines with existing Maze AI: study builder, AI moderator, and Maze MCP

⑤ Works alongside behavioral data (task success, paths, heatmaps) — same session, same participant

💡 Senior designer take

This is a methodology shift, not a feature add.

The two-phase research sprint — behavioral study first, qualitative deep-dive second — just became one. One recruitment round. One dataset. Results that arrive before the design is already locked.

Honest caveat: AI-moderated conversations are only as good as your question architecture. You still have to design what "why" means for your study. The AI handles the real-time branching. You handle the intent.

Researcher judgment doesn't go away. It moves earlier.

🎯 Real headline

Not "Maze added AI follow-ups."
It's "unmoderated research now has a 'why' layer in the same session."

The question isn't whether to use it.
It's whether you know how to design a conversation that earns honest answers.
