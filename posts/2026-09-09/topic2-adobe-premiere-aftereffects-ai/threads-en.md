# Topic 2: Adobe Premiere Generative Media Tool + After Effects AI Assistant (IBC 2026)
# Platform: Threads | Language: English
# Date: Sep 9, 2026 | Format: Long-form reflection
# Source: https://blog.adobe.com/en/publish/2026/09/08/generate-create-directly-in-your-timeline-with-new-ai-powered-innovations-in-premiere-after-effects (Sep 8, 2026)

---

Adobe shipped two AI updates at IBC this week — Premiere and After Effects. Worth separating them because they're solving different problems.

Premiere's Generative Media Tool is about filling gaps in your timeline. Draw a range, describe what needs to go there, choose from Firefly/Veo/Kling/Runway/Luma, generate it without leaving the app. The model picker shows credit cost before you commit — still rare in creative tools, and worth noting.

After Effects got something different: an AI Assistant that operates across your whole project, not just the composition you have open. It can reorganize massive inherited projects, fix broken expressions, and — most significantly — write expression rigs from plain language descriptions.

That last one matters. Expressions have always been the barrier in After Effects where you either learned JavaScript or accepted limits. "Build me a wiggle rig with adjustable sliders for amount, frequency and decay" is now a prompt, not an afternoon.

The honest framing here: After Effects accumulated ~30 years of capability and the resulting complexity is genuinely hard to navigate. The AI Assistant isn't replacing what motion designers are good at. It's building a navigation layer over the complexity they've always had to wrestle with.

Everything the Assistant does is undoable. It shows its tool calls. It explains its reasoning. That interaction model — transparent, revisable, show-your-work — is the one I trust.

Caveats: Video generation in Premiere carries credit costs (which stack up fast). After Effects Assistant is public beta. Generate Music and Soundscape are still in beta. Calibrate expectations accordingly.

The administrative overhead in motion design was always the thing that deserved to be automated first.

---

**Short take:**
Premiere fills gaps.
After Effects navigates 30 years of accumulated complexity.
Both are useful.
The expression writing is the one that actually changes the motion design floor.
