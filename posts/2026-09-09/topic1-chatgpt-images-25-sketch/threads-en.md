---

ChatGPT shipped a Sketch tool for image generation this week. You draw a quick visual reference — the AI uses that to generate.

I've been waiting for this one.

Not because the tool is impressive (it is). But because it fixes a fundamental mismatch that's been present in AI image generation since the beginning.

Text prompting is verbal. Design thinking is spatial.

When you describe a layout or visual direction to another person, you don't just say words. You gesture. You point. You draw a rough box and say "something like this, here." That's the natural mode of visual communication.

AI image generation made us regress. Everything went through text. Describe it precisely enough and hope the interpretation lands. For non-designers, that friction is small. For designers who have a clear spatial picture of what they need, it's been maddening.

@Sketch in ChatGPT lets you draw a rough reference — the AI generates from that. And for editing existing images, you can now annotate directly on the image where the change should go, instead of describing position in words.

Visual annotation as brief. The same shift Photoshop's AI Markup shipped two weeks ago. Now it's in the mainstream chat interface that 200M+ people use.

The honest caveat: brief quality is still the ceiling. You changed the format of the brief from text to sketch. Most designers sketch better than they describe. Not everyone will.

One more thing worth noting: OpenAI's PM on Images said "I don't want to be able to see ChatGPT in the world." Designed explicitly against aesthetic monoculture. AI that serves your visual language rather than expressing its own.

That's the right frame.

---

**Short take:**
Two years of text-only AI image prompting was a regression.
Designers think in space and gesture.
ChatGPT finally caught up to how we actually work.
