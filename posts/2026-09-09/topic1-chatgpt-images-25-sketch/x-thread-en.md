**Tweet 1 (Hook)**
ChatGPT just shipped a Sketch tool for image generation.

You draw a quick doodle. It generates from that.

"Sounds like a minor editing feature" is the wrong frame.

🧵

---

**Tweet 2**
What actually shipped in ChatGPT Images 2.5:

→ @Sketch: draw a visual reference, guide the generation from it
→ Direct annotation: mark where edits go on the image itself
→ 50% faster generation
→ Better likeness preservation (people, pets)
→ Text placement without re-prompting the whole image

That last bit about annotation is the one that matters.

---

**Tweet 3**
For two years, AI image generation ran entirely on text prompts.

Describe what you want. In words. Hope the model interprets it the way you meant.

That's a fundamentally verbal communication medium applied to a fundamentally visual problem.

Designers feel this mismatch immediately.

---

**Tweet 4**
Here's what visual annotation actually restores:

When a designer marks up a comp, they point at the thing.
"This corner. This spacing. This color, here."

That precision exists in spatial and gestural communication.
It was always the natural mode.

Text prompting made us regress to verbal-only.
Sketch brings spatial reasoning back.

---

**Tweet 5**
OpenAI's PM on Images said: "I don't want to be able to see ChatGPT in the world. I want people to generate their own individualism."

That's a deliberate design philosophy.

Not "train the model to have one consistent aesthetic."
Train the model to serve *your* visual language instead.

This is a direct acknowledgment of the AI monoculture problem.

---

**Tweet 6**
What changes in your workflow:

UI asset creation → annotate the region you want replaced
UX research stimuli → sketch layout, generate realistic content
Brand direction rounds → mark what's right, mark what's wrong
Presentation prep → sketch + generate, no stock photo hunting

The brief is still the ceiling. The format of the brief just got less verbal.

---

**Tweet 7**
Honest caveat:

The bottleneck didn't disappear. It changed format.

Text prompting requires: describe precisely.
Sketch prompting requires: draw precisely enough to communicate intent.

Most designers sketch better than they describe. Advantage shifts our way.
But brief quality is still the only variable that matters.

Sketch quality = output quality.
Same principle as design system quality = AI code quality.

Spatial annotation > verbal instruction.

ChatGPT Images 2.5 moved the mainstream interface to match what designers already knew.
