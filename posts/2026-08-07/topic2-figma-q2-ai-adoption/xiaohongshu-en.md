**Title:**
Figma's Q2 Stats Changed How I Think About My Design Career

---

**Body:**

Figma dropped Q2 2026 earnings yesterday. The headline is record revenue and raised guidance.

But one number buried in the investor report hit differently:

**80%+ of Figma enterprise customers are using AI credits every single week.**
50%+ are using the Figma agent weekly.

This changes the conversation.

———

**What 80% weekly usage actually means:**

This isn't early adoption. This isn't "some designers are experimenting."

When 80% of an enterprise customer base is using AI tools weekly, those tools have crossed into operational infrastructure — the same category as the design file itself.

The question is no longer "should we use AI?" It's "how do we govern AI use?"

———

**Why this matters for your career right now:**

If 80% of enterprise design orgs are using AI weekly, AI fluency is no longer a differentiator. It's a baseline expectation.

The new differentiator is being the designer who understands the **constraints**:
- What makes AI outputs reliable vs. unreliable
- What the quality ceilings are (design system quality, credit allocation)
- How to allocate AI usage toward high-value work vs. burning credits on things you could do faster yourself

That's a fundamentally different skill set than "prompting well."

———

**3 things worth reflecting on this week:**

1. **Track which of your workflows are AI-dependent** — If Figma's AI were unavailable for a week, which parts of your process would break down? That's your dependency map.

2. **Start building your ROI case for AI usage** — "I use AI a lot" is not going to hold up when your org starts asking about credit spend. "I use AI for X and it saves Y hours per sprint" is a real answer.

3. **Think about whether you're shaping or reacting** — In a team where 50%+ are using the Figma agent weekly, is there someone defining how AI fits into the design process? If not, that's a gap you could fill.

———

**Honest take:**

80% weekly usage doesn't mean AI is doing the design work.

It means the designers who understand how AI fits into good design work — and where it shouldn't — are in a structurally stronger position than those who don't.

The baseline moved. The question is where you want to be relative to it.
