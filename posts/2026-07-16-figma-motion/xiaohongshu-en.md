**Title:** Figma now has a timeline — what actually changed for UX designers

---

Figma Motion is live, and I've been testing it for the past few days as someone who designs component-heavy systems.

Here's my honest breakdown 👇

---

🎬 **What Figma Motion actually is**

A native animation timeline inside Figma Design. Not a plugin, not a separate tool — it's right there alongside Design, Draw, and Dev modes.

You get:
- Full keyframe control (position, scale, rotation, opacity)
- Easing curves + spring physics
- Preset animation styles (fade, move, scale)
- Auto-keyframe recording while you move the playhead

---

🔑 **The part that matters most for design systems**

You animate a component *once*, and that motion travels with it everywhere — the same way fill styles and typography tokens do.

This is motion becoming a **system property**.

Not a spec doc. Not a separate file. A token-equivalent layer of your design system.

---

👩‍💻 **The handoff story is actually good**

In Dev Mode:
- Full timeline is inspectable
- Every easing curve, timing value, keyframe is readable
- Copy directly as CSS, React, or JSON
- MCP-compatible → pass animated frames to coding agents with full context

No more "it should feel fast-ish" ambiguity.

---

✅ **Best use cases right now (it's still beta)**
1. Component-level micro-interactions
2. State change animations (hover, focus, error states)
3. Loading and transition animations within a single frame
4. Building a shared motion token library for your team

---

⚠️ **What's not ready yet**
- Screen-to-screen transitions (coming soon)
- Full spring physics refinement
- Lottie export (on the roadmap)

---

💭 **My take**

Motion has been a second-class citizen in design systems for too long. We tokenized everything *except* how things move.

Figma Motion doesn't just add animation. It asks your team a harder question:

**Are you ready to govern motion the same way you govern color and typography?**

That's the real design system conversation this unlocks.
