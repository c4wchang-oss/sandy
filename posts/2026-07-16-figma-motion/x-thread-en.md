# X Thread — Figma Motion (English)
**Topic:** Figma Motion: native timeline animation now live in Figma Design
**Angle:** Motion as a design system property, not a deliverable
**Format:** 8-tweet thread

---

**Tweet 1 (Hook)**
Figma Motion just shipped.

The obvious take: "designers don't need After Effects anymore."

The real headline is different. 🧵

---

**Tweet 2**
Figma Motion adds a full timeline to your canvas.

Keyframes. Easing curves. Spring physics. Preset animations.

Your components, your variables, your animations — all in the same file.

---

**Tweet 3**
But here's the part that actually changes how I think:

You animate a component *once*, and that motion travels with it everywhere — the same way fill styles and typography do.

That's motion as a **system property**, not a one-off deliverable.

---

**Tweet 4**
For 10+ years, motion has been a second-class citizen in design systems.

Colors → tokens. Spacing → tokens. Typography → tokens.

Motion → a separate After Effects file + a spec doc + a developer interpreting your "it should feel fast-ish" comment.

---

**Tweet 5**
In Dev Mode, the full timeline is inspectable.

Every timing value. Every easing curve. Every keyframe.

Developers copy directly in CSS, React, or JSON. No interpretation, no "what easing did you mean?"

---

**Tweet 6**
The pipeline shift that excites me most: Figma Motion is MCP-compatible.

You can pass any animated frame directly to a coding agent — with full keyframe and timing context attached.

Design intent → code, without the usual translation loss.

---

**Tweet 7**
Honest take: it's still beta.

Screen-to-screen transitions aren't ready. Full spring physics needs refinement.

Strongest right now for: component animations, micro-interactions, and state changes within a single frame.

---

**Tweet 8**
The question isn't "can I animate in Figma?"

It's: are you ready to treat motion as a **system property** — with the same rigor you give color and typography?

That's the design leadership question. And Figma just made it unavoidable.

→ figma.com/blog/introducing-figma-motion
