**Title:** Claude Design just changed — and Oct 15 is your deadline

**Body:**

If your team is on Claude enterprise, you have 3 days.

On October 15, Claude Design turns on by default for every enterprise organization. Here's what changed, what that means for you, and what you need to do before it activates.

**What's actually new in Claude Design:**

1️⃣ **Element-level editing controls** — Select anything on the canvas. Drag, resize, align directly. Inline text editing. Adjustment sliders for spacing and color. Undo/redo. This closes the "generate and lose control" gap. You can refine what AI makes, not just accept or reject it.

2️⃣ **/design in Claude Code** — Working in the terminal? Type /design. Claude generates artboards against your design system, you open the canvas in a browser, edit with full element control, and push straight back to Claude Code for implementation. Design now lives inside the dev workflow.

3️⃣ **14 connected export tools** — Adobe, Canva, Gamma, HyperFrames, Lovable, Miro, Netlify, Replit, v0, Vercel, Wix, Base44, Google Slides, and more. Your design system travels with the design to every destination.

4️⃣ **It's in every Claude conversation now** — Not just the standalone site. Any chat, any artifact, any coding session. The standalone site at claude.ai/design closes December 14 — if you built design systems there, migrate them now (9 weeks left).

**What you need to do before October 15:**

✅ Migrate your design system from claude.ai/design to Claude
✅ Upload organization guidelines for your team
✅ Decide who reviews AI-generated design work
✅ Brief your team on what this is and how to use it intentionally

**The bigger frame:**
The quality of what your org's Claude produces depends entirely on how good your design system documentation is. This is a DesignOps problem, not an IT problem.

"Accessible to everyone" ≠ "used well by everyone."

The governance gap opens the moment access becomes universal. October 15 is when that happens.
