# Threads Post — Inspo AI (English)

---

**Long-form version:**

I've been doing this long enough to know that the most expensive part of a design project isn't the design.

It's the two hours at the beginning of every project where you open five tabs, do keyword archaeology across Dribbble and Mobbin and Awwwards, collect 47 screenshots you'll never find again, and then try to turn that into a moodboard in a Google Doc that you send to a client who says "yeah I like this direction" without actually understanding the direction.

That loop costs teams hundreds of hours a year. And nobody talks about it because it doesn't feel like "real" design work. But it completely determines the quality of everything that comes after.

Inspo AI launched this week and it's trying to fix that loop. 50,000+ curated UI screens, searchable in plain-language design intent ("dark SaaS dashboard, editorial typography, generous whitespace"), flowing directly into collaborative moodboards you can annotate and share via link. No export. No rebuild. No screenshot chaos.

The part that actually interests me most is the brand audit: upload your brand guidelines, upload a design, get a score with visual annotations pointing at the exact color, type, and spacing violations. That's automated brand QA. That's a floor check before the review meeting.

Here's my honest take though: 50,000 screens only matters if the curation is fresh and deliberate. The comments questioning this on launch day are right to ask. The "inspired by" problem in AI design tools is real — when every team is referencing the same library of screens, you get interface convergence by committee. Reference quality isn't just about volume.

But the thesis underneath all of this is correct. Better references produce better briefs. Better briefs produce better AI output. The bottleneck was never the model. It's always been the input.

---

**Short punchy take:**

The design research phase is where projects get decided before anyone makes a single decision.

Inspo AI is betting that consolidating search, moodboard, and brand audit into one workflow is worth building a product around.

They're probably right. The question is whether the library stays fresh.
