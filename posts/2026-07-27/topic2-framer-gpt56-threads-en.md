# Topic 2: Framer 3.0 + GPT 5.6 — Threads Post (English)
**Date:** 2026-07-27
**Platform:** Threads
**Format:** Long-form personal reflection
**Angle:** Model tiering is a creative direction decision, not a pricing decision

---

Framer shipped a 25% credit efficiency improvement and made GPT 5.6 Terra the new default. The headline reads like a pricing footnote.

It's not.

Framer 3.0 has three GPT 5.6 model options: Sol (best quality, same credit cost as GPT 5.5), Terra (0.6x credits, balanced), Luna (0.4x credits, 2x faster). The efficiency update means multi-step builds now cost 60-67% fewer credits than at launch. That's a meaningful change to how you budget AI design work.

But the model menu is the real thing. Choosing which model runs on which task is a design workflow decision — not just a cost decision.

My current allocation: Sol for initial hero section ideation — this is the highest-judgment creative work, worth full credits. Terra for consistency passes and responsive breakpoints — I want systematic thinking, not creative exploration. Luna for bulk CMS updates and find-and-replace — speed matters, quality is already defined by the design system.

This is the same logic as staffing a team. You don't put your most expensive creative resource on writing every email subject line. The question is: can you classify your design tasks clearly enough to allocate correctly?

The bigger story in Framer 3.0 is External Agents. Claude Code connects to Framer via the Framer CLI, operates on branches automatically, and runs on your own LLM tokens — not Framer credits. Framer becomes a deployment target in your Claude Code pipeline.

That's a significant architectural shift. The design tool isn't where design starts. It's where the AI lands. You review on canvas. The agent is the author. Senior designers manage the Branching strategy and the model allocation policy — not the mouse clicks.

If you're already using Claude Code: try External Agents in Framer. If you're not: start with Terra for a site-wide consistency pass. Most practical, most credit-efficient place to start.
