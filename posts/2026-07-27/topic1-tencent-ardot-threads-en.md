# Topic 1: Tencent Ardot Beta — Threads Post (English)
**Date:** 2026-07-27
**Platform:** Threads
**Format:** Long-form personal reflection
**Angle:** Three people on one canvas — and what the designer's role becomes

---

Three people looking at the same canvas at the same time. That's the actual product announcement from Tencent's Ardot beta.

Not "AI generates UI faster." Not "PM skips the designer." The pitch is simultaneous co-presence: PM describes requirements, designer refines, developer previews code — all on one canvas in real time.

I've been a designer long enough to know what most of my job actually is. Not designing. Translating. PM requirement → design intent → developer question → back to design intent. Most of that translation loop exists because we work in different tools at different phases.

If AI collapses those phases into one canvas, the translation shrinks. And with it, a chunk of what makes "being a designer" distinct from "everyone else who has opinions about the interface."

The comfortable read: designers get to focus on what matters — judgment, creativity, experience quality.

The uncomfortable read: "focus on creativity" usually means junior roles absorb the commodity work first. The first thing to disappear isn't the design role. It's junior headcount.

What I'm actually testing when I try Ardot: whether the one-click React/Vue export holds up against a real design system. Generating something that looks like a component library is easy. Generating something that actually is — token-aligned, variant-complete, constraint-aware — that's still hard.

The handoff didn't disappear. It moved. Now it's between AI output and production-ready code. That gap is still where quality lives.

Ardot is live in beta (1,000 free credits). If you test it, don't test against a blank canvas. Test against your actual design system and see if the code respects your constraints. That's the real review.
