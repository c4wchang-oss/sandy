## Threads — English

Canva AI 2.0 launched Memory Library this week, and I've been sitting with a specific question since I read through it.

The feature: persistent memory that learns how you work across sessions — brand voice, spacing habits, creative style — and applies it automatically. You stop re-establishing context every time you open a new file. For a 200M+ user platform, that's a meaningful quality floor raise.

But here's the thing that's been nagging at me.

Memory is opaque in one direction. It accumulates what you *did*, not what you *intended*. Style drift — the slow, invisible accumulation of inconsistencies — becomes impossible to notice when AI is doing the remembering for you.

In a solo workflow, that's manageable. In a team? Whose preferences are being encoded? The most active user? The first person to onboard? The marketer who ran 40 campaigns before the designer joined? Memory Library doesn't answer that question. It doesn't need to. That's a governance design problem — your governance design problem.

The harder design principle question: what happens when you want the brand to *evolve*? You used to update the guidelines. Now you also have to audit what the system quietly learned — and override it deliberately. Style evolution is no longer passive. It became an intentional act.

Canva AI 2.0 is in research preview, rolling out over the coming weeks. Full documentation on memory accumulation, reset controls, and team-level management isn't public yet. Test before trusting it with production brand assets.

The governance principle from design systems hasn't changed: the rules AI operates by are only as good as the intent behind them. The new part is that some of those rules are ones AI wrote itself.
