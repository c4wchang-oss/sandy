**Tweet 1 (Hook)**
Every qual researcher knows the feeling:

You've done the analysis. The insights are solid. Then you spend two evenings rebuilding them into slides.

flowres.io just shipped SlideBuilder. It's not another "one-click presentation" tool. And that's exactly why it's interesting.

🧵

---

**Tweet 2**
There are two different problems in qual research reporting:

1. Generating slides (Gamma, Beautiful.ai, Claude Design do this)
2. Getting your analyzed evidence into a deliverable without rebuilding it from scratch

Most AI presentation tools solve #1.

SlideBuilder solves #2.

---

**Tweet 3**
The structural disconnect they're fixing:

Evidence lives in your analysis tool.
Deliverables get built in PowerPoint.
The translation between them is a manual rebuild.

That rebuild is where hours go.
Not to creativity. Not to shaping the narrative.
Just recreating work you already did.

---

**Tweet 4**
How SlideBuilder works:

1. Analyze in flowres.io, bookmark what matters (themes, quotes, summaries)
2. Open PowerPoint, launch the add-in
3. Browse your bookmarked evidence directly inside PowerPoint
4. Select what goes in, generate slides into your existing template
5. Slides are fully editable native PowerPoint elements

No tab-switching. No copy-paste. Your analysis becomes your deck.

---

**Tweet 5**
The explicit positioning is sharp:

"Prompt to PPT" = AI creates from scratch, researcher describes what they want
"Data to editable PPT" = AI scaffolds from evidence the researcher already curated

Different product category. Different quality floor.

---

**Tweet 6**
What I find honest about this:

They explicitly say SlideBuilder doesn't replace Gamma or Claude Design.
Once the content is in the deck, those tools still help you polish and restructure.

Infrastructure + generator = both have a place. That's a real workflow, not marketing.

---

**Tweet 7**
The caveat worth noting:

→ PowerPoint only (no Google Slides yet)
→ Requires flowres.io account + completed analysis
→ US, UK, EU only currently
→ The bottleneck shifts — now the quality ceiling is your bookmark curation, not slide speed

If you bookmark vague summaries, you get vague slides.
The researcher's judgment is still the input.

---

**Tweet 8 (CTA)**
The role hasn't changed.

The researcher still decides what goes in, how the narrative flows, what the evidence actually means.

What changed: the rebuild step between "analysis done" and "deck done" is finally automated.

That's not replacing research judgment.
That's respecting it.
