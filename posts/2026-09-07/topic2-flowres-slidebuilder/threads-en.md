## Long-form Post

The part of qualitative research that AI tools kept skipping over: the rebuild.

You finish your analysis. You have themes, quotes, summaries, patterns — real evidence, hard-won. Then you open a new PowerPoint, look at the blank slide, and start re-entering everything you just finished thinking through. That's not storytelling. That's transcription. And it's where the late evenings go.

AI presentation tools (Gamma, Beautiful.ai, Claude Design, others) solved a different problem: generating slides from a prompt or a document. That's actually useful for many things. But for qual researchers, the bottleneck was never "how do I make slides faster?" It was "why do I have to rebuild my analysis into a completely different format before I can make slides at all?"

flowres.io shipped SlideBuilder. It's a PowerPoint add-in that lets you browse your analyzed evidence — bookmarked summaries, themes, quotes from your flowres.io project — directly inside PowerPoint and generate slides into your existing template. The slides are native PowerPoint elements. Your template stays intact. You decide what goes in and in what order.

They're explicit about what it's not: not a one-click presentation maker, not a replacement for Gamma or Claude Design. Those tools still have a role once the research content is already in the deck. SlideBuilder handles the specific gap: from "analysis complete" to "evidence in a deck," which is where the structural disconnect was.

The distinction they use is worth keeping: "Prompt to PPT" vs. "Data to editable PPT." These are different products for different bottlenecks. One generates from description. One transfers from evidence. Mixing them up is how you end up with AI tools that feel useful for demos but wrong for real deliverables.

The honest limitation: the quality ceiling is now your bookmark curation. If you tag vague summaries in flowres.io, you get vague slides. The researcher's judgment is still the input. That's actually the right design — it respects where human judgment belongs (what the evidence means and how the narrative should flow) while automating what it doesn't (the manual transfer between tools).

We've had AI tools accelerate the generation side of research for a while now. This is one of the first tools I've seen that addresses the reporting side without pretending to replace the researcher.

## Short Punchy Take

The qual research reporting bottleneck isn't making slides.

It's the manual rebuild — analysis done in one tool, deliverable built in another.

SlideBuilder brings the evidence to where the deck lives.
Not a slide generator. Infrastructure.
