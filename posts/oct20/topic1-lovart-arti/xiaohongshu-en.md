## XIAOHONGSHU POST (English)

**Title:** Lovart 2.0: The AI design agent that learns who you are (and why that's more complicated than it sounds)

**Body:**

Lovart 2.0 launched Arti on Oct 19 — a personal design agent with persistent memory, scheduled tasks, and self-evolving skills. Here's what actually matters, and where to be careful. (Senior UX take, 10+ years in.)

**What's genuinely new:**

1. Memory organized by type: aesthetics, collaboration style, assets, basics. You can edit or delete every entry. Your profile can stay per-project or travel globally.

2. Design delegation: write a brief → set a schedule (daily/weekly/monthly) → set a credit limit → test on 4 items → let it execute. Results land on a canvas page for you to review.

3. Self-evolving skills: use the same workflow 3× → Lovart extracts it as a reusable skill automatically. Pattern learning from your behavior — not from writing a skill doc.

4. Design Review: consistency checker that runs quietly in background, flags issues (like an oil-paint texture inside a hand-drawn animation set), waits for your confirmation before fixing.

5. Feedback anchored to elements: reviewers annotate specific elements → you bring comments into the conversation with context → agent proposes a revision plan → you confirm.

**The honest limitation:**

Your memory profile is now a quality ceiling. Outdated aesthetic preferences in Arti = consistent, silent, scaled degradation across your work. The brief-quality problem moved from per-task to per-identity. Review your Arti profile regularly — treat it like a design brief that can go stale.

**Who this matters for most:**

— E-commerce designers running weekly batch production (42 SKUs overnight? Yes.)
— Brand designers maintaining consistency across long-running campaigns
— Senior designers who want to delegate structured, repeatable tasks without losing stylistic control

**My take:**

Design tools have had AI assistants. They haven't had memory. The interesting design question is whether a tool can learn *the right things* about you — and whether you can govern what it learns.
