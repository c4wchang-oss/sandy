**Tweet 2**
Built by Andriy (ex-Apple, GitLab, Mixpanel, Head of Design at Product Hunt) and Dmitriy (co-founder of GitLab).

Their shared frustration: AI design tools hand you a pretty mockup and call it done.

So they built handoffs instead.

---

**Tweet 3**
Three specialized agents. Real artifact at each stage.

Elena → research
Marcus → 3 creative direction pitches, argues for the strongest
Mira → wireframes + production HTML with a design system

25–45 minutes per brief. You bring your OpenRouter key.

---

**Tweet 4**
Marcus "argues for the strongest direction" is the line that got me.

Not outputting 3 options and asking you to pick.
The agent makes a case.

That's a taste decision the model is making on your behalf.
Worth paying close attention to which direction "wins."

---

**Tweet 5**
The "watch agents discuss decisions" feature is underrated.

Most AI tools hide the process. Showing deliberation is a trust interface.

You calibrate trust by seeing judgment applied, not just seeing results.

Same reason code review works better than just shipping what Cursor generated.

---

**Tweet 6**
Honest caveats:
→ Free alpha, bring your own OpenRouter key
→ 25–45 min per brief — not instant
→ Research quality depends on Elena's access
→ "Production HTML" quality still scales with brief clarity

Not magic. But the structure is right.

---

**Tweet 7**
A multi-agent crew with specialized roles and real handoffs will outperform a one-prompt generalist.

Every time.

Same reason a well-structured team outperforms a single brilliant person at scale.

---

**Tweet 8 — CTA**
The question isn't "can agents design?"

It's "do you understand the stages well enough to evaluate what each agent produced?"

Senior designers know where in the pipeline the handoff broke down.

That judgment doesn't go away. It gets more important.
