# Threads Post — Canva Code 2.0 (English)
**Format:** Long-form personal reflection, no hashtags

---

Something shifted this week in how AI tools and design tools are going to coexist — and it happened quietly inside Canva Code 2.0's launch.

Here's what I can't stop thinking about:

Canva isn't competing with Lovable or Bolt. It's positioning itself as the layer *after* those tools. The HTML import feature lets you take anything generated by any AI tool — ChatGPT, Claude, Lovable, Bolt — and pull it into Canva as editable design layers. They just made themselves the finishing layer for every vibe coding workflow on the market.

And it makes complete sense from a product design perspective. Every other vibe coding tool optimizes for *getting to functional*. Canva's bet is that functional isn't enough — brand fidelity and visual polish are the real bottleneck for most users. They built the editing surface first, then added AI generation as the entry point. That's a fundamentally different product architecture than their competitors.

The feature I keep coming back to: you can click directly into any generated element to change text, drag in images from Canva's library, update colors through a familiar toolbar — without touching the AI again. Compare that to every other tool in this space, where "make this look more like my brand" means going back to the prompt. Canva solved the editing problem first. Generation was always step two.

For UX designers, the interesting shift is this: AI generation is getting commoditized. Every tool can spin up a working prototype from a text prompt now. The quality of the editing surface *around* that generation is where differentiation is starting to land. Canva figured that out before most.

Honest limitations worth naming: Canva Code is a front-end tool at modest scale. Their own team said it directly — not for complex backends or high-traffic production apps. That constraint isn't a weakness. It's a deliberate focus on the audience Canva has always served: the teachers, marketers, and small business owners who need something that looks professional and works on mobile, without hiring a developer.

The real headline from this week: Canva made design-quality a non-optional step in the AI build process. For 265 million people, for free. That's a bigger move than it looks.
